Abstract —Started as SMS ticketing, today, mobile ticketing 
services become a platform for combined mobility solutions that integrate mobile ticketing, bike renting, car sharing, and parking payment services. Mobile ticketing services provide a number of benefits for public transport companies and passengers, however, in some Northern European countries, the share of mobile ticket sales does not exceed 10 percent. In this paper, we analyse business models of three types of mobile ticketing services (SMS, 
in-app, and “Be-In Be-Out” (BIBO )) in order to identify factors 
that negatively affect wider penetration of mobile ticketing. In order to do so, we use STOF (Service, Technology, Organisation, and Finance) model. The research reveals that there are a number of challenges related to service, technology, and finance domains of mobile ticketing. Due to this, with the help of price 
policy, many public transport companies push choices of their 
customers towards use of plastic travel card.  
Keywords—mobile ticketing; business model; STOF 
I.  INTRODUCTION  
Public transport is an important area that is highly affecting 
everyday life of citizens. Mobile ticketing services contribute 
to making public transport smarter and have a potential to 
reach wide scale penetration, since many people use public 
transport. Mobile ticketing started as premium SMS services 
for single tickets. With wider smartphone penetration mobile 
ticketing applications were introduced. The first apps did offer 
single tickets only and targeted  non-regular public transport 
passengers having no travel card. Now, the majority of 
ticketing apps offers almost a full range of available tickets for 
all types of passengers. Notable trends in mobile ticketing are 
introduction of “Be-In Be-Out ” (BIBO) seamless ticketing 
solutions, contactless payments, and combined mobility 
solutions that integrate mobile ticketing services, bike renting, 
car sharing, parking, etc. 
In some Northern Europe countries mobile ticketing 
solutions became available starting from 2009 and were 
commonly used by public transport users. In addition, 
different types of mobile ticketing and payment solutions have 
been developed and used in these countries. For this reason, it would be interesting to investigate if this successful 
application of mobile services may have business model challenges. With exception of a study by Juntunen et al. [1], 
[2] focused on critical business model issues of Near Field 
Communication (NFC) based mobile  ticketing in Finland, to 
the authors knowledge, there were no other studies addressing 
similar questions. We address this research gap and explore 
obstacles and challenges associated with business model (or 
critical business model issues) of mobile ticketing services in 
five Northern European countries (Sweden, Norway, Estonia, 
Denmark, and Lithuania). This paper addresses the following 
research question: What are the factors negatively affecting 
the penetration of mobile ticketing services? In order to 
answer this research question, we applied the STOF (Service, 
Technology, Organisation, and Fi nance) model [25], [27] and 
analysed issues related w ith each of its elements.  
The major theoretical contributions of this study are (1) 
application of the STOF framew ork for analysis of existing 
business models in order to id entify key challenges of mobile 
ticketing services and (2) extens ion of knowledge about factors 
that affect the scale of mobile ticketing penetration. In addition, 
the research findings might be of interest for practitioners by 
(1) providing generalised data on mobile ticketing service 
challenges in a number of countries and (2) limitations of 
existing mobile ticketing services.  
Next we present a brief overview of related work. This is 
followed by description of methodology and mobile ticketing 
services. Then we present findi ngs and perform a comparative 
analysis. Finally, we discuss the results, research contributions 
and limitations.

B. Business Model Concept 
The business model concept became a highly popular topic 
of the research interest in the past decade. This can be 
exemplified by the following figures: more than 1000 papers 
were dedicated to business model since 1995 [16]. One of the 
factors explaining popularity of this  topic can be related to the 
boom of e-commerce [17]. Despite a considerable amount of 
research in the area, currently , there is no commonly accepted 
definition of business model. As a consequence, numerous 
definitions and elements of business model have been 
proposed. This is discussed below.  
1) Business Model: Definition 
A review of literature on business models provides various 
definitions. For example,  business model is a “ narrative 
device ” that defines context and links parties across the 
network [18]. According to another definition, business model 
interrelates elements of strategy, resource allocation, organizational activities, and architecture [17].  
A number of business model definitions are related to the 
question of value provided bot h to consumers and market 
actors. From a wider perspective, a business model can be 
defined as “a blueprint for the way a business creates and 
captures value from new services or products” [19]. In a 
number of theoretical studies, the question of value creation 
and proposition is considered as one of the most essential 
questions any business model should address [20], [21], [22]. 
For example, a business model is a concept depicting how 
value is created through transactions [20].  
In summary, a business model describes value creation and 
delivery aspects. In addition it incorporates elements of 
corporate strategy, management  of resource allocation, and 
organizational management.  
2) Business Model: The Main Components 
The majority of business model definitions seek to define 
its structural components. Diffe rent authors [17], [21], [23], 
[24], [25] emphasise different components of business model. Most of authors agree that important components are (1) a 
service or a product and its value proposition; (2) target 
customers; (3) value network of actors that provide this product 
or service, relationships w ithin the network, and core 
competencies of actors; (4) fi nancial aspects that include 
revenue and cost models. Based on extensive literature review, 
researchers [26] classify three groups of components: strategic, 
customer and market, and value creation. Each of these 
components is divided into three models that address different 
component’s aspects.  
C.
 Theoretical Framework 
For the purpose of this research, we use the STOF model 
(Fig. 1) [25], [27]. This mode l is specifically adapted for 
analysis of mobile services that require collaboration of 
different actors [25]. The STOF model was used in studies on 
mobile payments [28], mobile ticketing services [1], [2], 
location based services [29], and eHealth [30]. Below, we 
discuss this model and its four components (service, 
technology, organisation, and fina nce domains) in more details. 
The analysis of the service domain  is focused on the key 
service aspects: mobile service offering, value proposition or 
added value services, and the target market segment. Hence, 
“value” of a service is the central concept. Value can be 
defined as “the perceived benefits and total costs (or sacrifice) 
of (obtaining) a product or serv ice for customers in target 
markets [27]. For mobile ticketing services, mobility “includes 
time and place independent service access, reduced queuing, 
and substituting for other services” [11].  
The technology domain is focused on “the technical 
functionality requited to realize the service offering” [25]. 
Functionality is related to technical systems and architectures, 
applications, devices, service platforms and required 
infrastructure, service s ecurity, quality, operability, 
accessibility by customers [2], [25], [27].  
The main focus of the  organisation domain  is on resources 
and capabilities of a firm and value network created for service provision. Other important aspe cts are division of roles among 
business actors, relations between them, resources and 
capabilities of actors, and value network governance [25], [27].

III. METODOLOGY  
A. Research Approach 
In this study, we apply an expl oratory approach in order to 
identify challenges related to business models of mobile 
ticketing services. The research is qualitative. Based on used 
technology, we specified three types of mobile ticketing 
services: SMS, in-app, and BIBO. Description of these services 
is generalised using empiric data  from mobile ticketing cases in 
five Northern European countries. Use of multiple case studies 
is more beneficial than single case study because it enables 
cross-case analysis and more accurate generalization of 
findings [34], [35]. 
In order to perform multiple case study research, we 
developed a case study protocol. The aim of this document is to 
define general rules and procedures of the research [35]. This 
allowed a unified approach in data collection.  
B. Data Collection and Analysis 
The primary data for case studies was collected using in-
depth semi-structured interviews  with the representatives of 
companies, which are involved in  provision of mobile ticketing 
services. First of all, an interv iew protocol with questions for 
semi-structured interviews was designed. The questions 
covered the following interview subjects: (1) the background of 
the interviewee and the company; (2) the history of the mobile 
payment ticketing service and service characteristics; (3) 
description of the main partners and their roles; (4) advantages 
and challenges of the service; and (5) general feedback from 
the end-users. Contacted interviewees were middle- or top-
level managers that are directly involved in mobile ticketing 
service management. Hence, interviewees’ expertise was 
relevant to provided detailed information. The duration of 
interviews was between one and two hours. The interviews 
were recorded and later transcribed.  
The interviews were implemented in two phases. In 2010-
2013, the main research focus was on Sweden. During these 
years, more than 30 interviews were carried out with different 
types of actors. This includes:  (1) mobile network operators 
(Telia and Tre); (2) Swedish major regional public transport 
companies (SL, UL, Östgötat rafiken, Skånetrafiken, 
Västtrafik); (3) payment processors (Accumulate, Payair, 
Nets, PayEx, Seamless, WyWallet); (4) a bank (Swedbank); 
and (5) providers of mobile ticket technology solutions 
(UnWire, Mobill, and Samtrafiken). These results were 
reported in [33]. 
In 2015-2016, the research was broadened to cover other 
countries than Sweden. More than 20 interviews were 
conducted. The list of interviewe d actors involved: (1) mobile 
network operators (in Lithuani a: Bite Lietuva); (2) public 
transport companies (in Sweden : SL, UL, Östgötatrafiken, 
Skånetrafiken, Västtrafik, Blekingetrafiken, Länstrafiken 
Kronaberg, Karlstadbuss; in Norway: Ruter and Skyss; in 
Denmark: Copenhagen Metro a nd Movia; in Lithuania: 
Susisiekimo Paslaugos); (3) pa yment processors (in Sweden: PayEx and WyWallet); (4) banks (in Norway: DNB; in 
Denmark: Danske Bank); (5) providers of ticketing solution for 
transport companies (in Estoni a: Jiffi and Ridango); and (6) 
city administration (in Lithuania: Vilnius city administration).  
We used secondary data in order to understand the 
background situation in the market. The main used sources are 
press releases, market analysis reports, websites, and online 
publications.  
As mentioned, descriptions of SMS, in-app, and BIBO 
ticketing services are supported by empiric data from five 
Northern European countries . When analysing data, the 
empirical observations were cla ssified using the STOF model. 
This is, challenges and issues specified in the interviews were 
classified into issues speci fically related to service, 
technology, organisation, and fi nance domains. We performed 
comparative analysis in order to identify similarities and 
differences across different mobile ticketing services.  
IV. MOBILE TICKETING SERVICES  
There are two main strategies for public transportation 
ticketing and validation: (i) barrier ticketing, where customers 
have to provide a ticket to someone or something (e.g. bus 
driver or ticket reader in the subway), and (ii) an open system, 
where passengers enter the underground, busses, trams, or 
boats without validating the ticket, but they need to provide a 
ticket during random ticket control inspections. Below, we 
present different mobile  ticketing services.  
A. SMS ticketing service 
1) Premium SMS based ticketing service 
In order to describe the business model of SMS ticketing 
services, we synthesised data based on interviews with public 
transport companies operating in Sweden (Bleningetrafiken, 
Karlstadsbuss, Skånetrafiken, SL, UL, Östgötatrafiken, and 
Västtrafik) and in Copenhagen area and Zealand in Denmark (a 
common solution used by Copenhagen Metro and Movia, 
authority regulating bus serv ice and local trains).  
SMS ticketing was commercially launched by local public 
transport companies in Sweden and Copenhagen around 2009. 
In order to get the ticket cust omers need to send a premium 
SMS with a service code. The service can be used for both pre-
paid mobile phone accounts and mobile phone subscriptions. 
Local mobile network operators include payments for SMS 
tickets in their customers’ mob ile phone subscription bills. The 
service is simple and highly popular among public transport 
passengers. The majority of sold SMS tickets are single tickets. 
For service provision, two type s of actors are needed: SMS 
aggregators and ticket providers (e.g. Plusdial, UnWire, 
Mobill, and IPX in different regions of Sweden, and UnWire in 
Copenhagen) that provide t echnology solution, and mobile 
network operators that act as payment processors.  
In Sweden, SMS ticketing was available until February 
2013. In Copenhagen, the SMS tickets are still available today. 
2) “New” SMS ticketing service in Sweden 
In Sweden, the SMS ticketing service was affected by 
changes in regulation: (1) a licen se became mandatory in order

The major change related to “new” SMS ticketing solutions 
was a need for customer to register and open an account with 
payment service providers. Service users accepted this 
negatively and volumes of SMS ticket sales dramatically 
decreased. Possible billing options  were monthly invoices or 
credit/debit card payments. In a few years, the “new” SMS 
ticketing got a form of mobile a pp. Majority of public transport 
companies closed this solution during 2016. 
B. In-app mobile ticketing service  
Description of in-app mobile ticketing service is 
generalised based on interviews with public transport 
companies operating in Sw eden (Bleningetrafiken, 
Karlstadsbuss, Länstrafiken Kr onaberg, Skånetrafiken, SL, UL, 
Östgötatrafiken, and Västtrafik ), Norway (Ruter and Skyss), 
Copenhagen area and Zealand in Denmark (DOT 
Mobilbilletter, an app for single tickets, short period tickets, 
and 10-trips cards, and Mobilperiodekort, an app for long 
period passes, two common solutions used by Copenhagen 
Metro and Movia), Estonia (Rida ngo), and Lithuania (mTicket 
app by Susisiekimo Paslaugos).  
Around 2013–2014 public transport companies started 
introduction of mobile ticketing apps. First, only single tickets 
were available in these apps. Currently (by 2017), the majority 
of apps offer partial or almost full range of period tickets 
(Karlsradsbuss, Skånetrafiken, UL, Västtrafik, Ruter, Skyss, 
DOT Mobilbilletter, Mobilperiode kort, and mTicket), while a 
few companies (Bleningetrafiken, Länstrafiken Kronaberg, 
Östgötatrafiken, SL, and Ri dango) still provide only single 
tickets in the app. Available payment options are card 
payments, invoices (mainly in  Sweden), mobile network 
operator billing (Norway, Denmark, and Estonia), mobile 
payment services (Sweden, Norway, and Denmark), and  online payment solution “Bank Link” offered by local banks 
(Estonia and Lithuania).  
Majority of mobile ticketing apps are created for Android 
and iPhone phones. The segment of Windows phone users is 
very small, and in the majority of cases this group of 
customers is not considered. 
Mobile ticketing allows additional services. Route planning 
and real-time travel information are the most common. Unique 
additional services are monthly pass renewal reminder 
(Mobileperiodekort app), digita l ticket sharing (Västtrafik 
app), and city map (mTicket).  
The provision of mobile ticketing requires a technical 
solution integrated with public transport system and payment 
service provider. In different c ountries different market actors 
perform a role of payment service provider. This could be 
payment processors handling invoice, card, and mobile 
payment service transactions (Sweden, Norway, and 
Denmark), mobile operators (Norway, Denmark, and Estonia), 
and banks (Estonia and Lithuania).  
In closed public transport sy stems (e.g. Sweden), mobile 
ticket validation requires optical readers. In open public 
transport system (in Sweden it is only Gothenburg (operated by 
Västtrafik), Norway, Denmark, Es tonia, and Lithuania) mobile 
tickets are validated by ticket inspectors during random ticket 
control, and no service infrastructure is needed.  
C. BIBO ticketing service 
Description of BIBO ticketing service is generalised based 
on interviews with Ruter in Norway and Jiffi in Estonia. This 
is as innovative approach to ticketing. It represents an 
automated fare collection system based on BLE beacons and 
micro-location technology. Th is technology detects when 
passengers enter and exit the public transit vehicle and 
seamlessly starts the ticket on the ticketing app, and stops when 
the customer leaves the bus. Customer billing happens 
automatically according to the actual use of the public 
transport.  
Ruter runs a pilot tests on one of bus lines in Oslo, and Jiffi 
pilots are running in Tallinn and Tartu. The major advantage of 
such system is a clear data a bout passenger travels that would 
help to improve and optimis e route planning. Another 
advantage is smarter pricing. 
V. FINDINGS  
Findings are generalised based on interviews with all 
interviewees. This is presented below. 
A. Business Model challenges: SMS and “new” SMS ticketing 
1) Service domain.  
Small mobile ticket assortment . SMS tickets were mainly 
single tickets (in Sweden and in the beginning in Denmark).  
SMS tickets are not available for foreigners. SMS tickets 
can only be bought by people having subscription with local mobile operators. People with mobile phone subscription in 
other countries (e.g. tourists) cannot buy these tickets.

Inconvenient service . The “new” SMS tickets were 
complicated for users to sign-up and were not convenient to 
use. 
2) Technology domain. 
Service reliability is high, but not 100 percent . Visitors 
arriving to Copenhagen can buy a Travel Pass or a City Pass 
before they arrive. However, sometimes the tourists or the 
delegates of the congress do not get the SMS ticket.  
Worse travelling statistics . In both open and closed 
ticketing systems, SMS ticketing solution does not provide 
travel data as is some cases they are just shown to the driver 
when entering a bus, to personnel at gates, or to the ticket 
inspectors. And travelling statistics is important for public 
transport companies in order to optimise the routes.  
Ticket security issues . SMS ticket are easy to falsify. In 
addition, these tickets look different from phone to phone. 
3) Organisation domain. 
Change of value network actors . In Sweden, the unified 
SMS ticketing solution was provided in collaboration with 
national mobile network operators , which are large, established 
market actors. After launch of  “new” SMS ticketing, unified 
solution was replaced by different scattered solutions, and 
mobile network operators were replaced by small technology 
companies. This implies weaker  relationships between business 
parties.  
However, in all cases, roles of actors are clearly defined. 
These actors are contracted through procurement process. 
4) Financial domain. 
Service cost . The mobile operators’ service fee is 
considered as quite high (about  15–30%) and SMS tickets are 
referred as more expensive compared to the mobile app 
tickets.  
B. Business Model challenges: In-app ticketing 
1) Service domain.  
Small mobile ticket assortment . In the beginning, majority 
of mobile ticket apps only allowed purchase of single tickets 
(e.g. SL, Skånetrafiken, Länstrafiken Kronoberg). This is 
related to a lack of automatic  mobile ticket validation.  
With installation of mobile tic ket readers, recently updated 
apps of some companies (Karlstadbuss, Skånetrafiken, and Västtrafik) started offering longer period tickets, for example, 
24h and 72h tickets, 30- and 90-day. 
Some groups of customers can be excluded . The SMS 
ticketing was accessible for people with both smartphones and 
feature phones. Customers usi ng feature phones cannot buy an 
app ticket. Majority of companies do not provide apps for 
Windows phone users due to small share of these phones in the 
market.  
Small market share . In Sweden, after not successful 
experience of “new” SMS ticketing, the share of mobile 
ticketing remains rather low, for example, 3% of all sales at 
SL, 5% at Skånetrafiken, 7% at Västtrafik (in 2016). Due to 
limited payment options, the market share of mTicket in 
Lithuania is 3%.  Some groups of customers do not want to use mobile app . 
Customers may not want to use mobile app due to different 
reasons. They may not want to change or be afraid that the 
phone will run out of battery. Additionally, in some countries 
(e.g. Sweden and Norway) travel cards are not personalised 
and can be shared between several people. These groups of 
people want to continue using plastic cards.  
2) Technology domain. 
Transaction speed.  The speed of payment validation is 
critical for transportation in large cities where big numbers of 
people pass through the subway gates. NFC-enabled travel 
cards can ensure this speed (it is about 400 milliseconds). 
Payments taking longer time a nd requiring PIN-code entering 
are not acceptable at gates.  
Need for infrastructure. 
In Sweden, most of the apps only 
offered single tickets, and some still offer single tickets today 
(e.g. SL, Bleningetrafiken, Ös tgötatrafiken, Länstrafiken 
Kronaberg). Due to closed ticketing system, an infrastructure 
for automatic mobile ticket valida tion is needed. It would also 
provide statistics on customer tr avels. Companies are installing 
or have plans to install optical  readers in the near future.  
Worse travelling statistics . In both open and closed 
ticketing systems, in-app mob ile ticketing solutions do not 
provide travel data for the same reasons as SMS tickets. As 
mentioned, installation of mobile ticket readers will improve 
the situation. Another option is introduction of buses with 
passengers’ counting systems (Vilnius City  Councilor). 
Special challenge for a large cit y (only SL issue ). The 
ticketing system in Stockholm is closed: a ticket is validated at 
the gates in the underground or showing a ticket to a driver. 
Ticketing system based on card is a stable offline system. The mobile ticketing system is based on online connection and it 
requires a system able to validate the tickets online. It is 
challenging to build a reliable online system, there might be 
moment when the system will be offline, payments information 
will not be handled, and this will result in issues validating the 
tickets at the gates. At this moment, the company cannot 
handle large numbers of mobile tickets. This issue will be 
solves when new validation equipment is installed. 
Security of user accounts . The ticketing system or its part 
(e.g. user accounts) can be hacked (as happened to Skyss).  
Discharging phone battery . One of the biggest 
disadvantages of mobile ticketing is a quickly discharging 
battery. This is a reason preventing some people from using in-
app mobile ticketing (19% according to Ruter’s survey). The 
public transport companies consider that it is the customer 
responsibility to have charged phone and to be able to show the 
ticket. However, in order to help their customers, in some cases 
ticket control inspectors have portable charges, but their 
experience shows that the phones are charging too slow and 
this practice is not successful. One way to solve this problem is 
to have the opportunity to charge phones on-board.  
3) Organisation domain. 
There were no challenges identified. Roles of actors are 
clearly defined. These act ors are contracted through 
procurement process.

4) Financial domain. 
Service cost . Some respondents (SL, Skyss, Jiffi) specified 
mobile tickets in the app as having a higher cost. Generalising, 
the major sources of costs for in app ticketing are: (i) higher 
percentage of commissions; and (ii) service fees set by 
payment service providers and/or  technical solution providers. 
With the help of price policy, most of public transport 
companies in Sweden direct the customer choice towards use 
of pre-paid smart cards (e.g . single ticket in Stockholm bought 
with a card costs 30 SEK, and 43 SEK with mobile app, 
Blekingetrafiken and Länstrafiken Kronaberg offer 20% and 
Skånetrafiken offers 10% discount  if buying single tickets with 
pre-paid card). Similar approach is in Copenhagen (about 40% 
discount buying 10 trip card using pre-paid card). It is also 
considered that customers who have loaded money to the card 
will be returning public transportation customers. 
Credit card fraud . Credit card fraud is another problem of 
mobile ticketing. In some cases 3D card security does not work 
in the app and public transport companies endure financial 
risks, which can be rather high in the case of longer period 
tickets (e.g. 90 days or yearly passes). From this perspective, 
plastic travel cards have better security and no fraud.  
C. Business Model challenges: BIBO ticketing 
1) Service domain.  
Some groups of customers can be excluded . Currently, 
BIBO systems are in the phase of pilot tests, however, since 
they are designed for smartphones, customers using feature 
phones will not be able to use this system.  
2) Technology domain.  
Smartphone characteristics . Smartphones are not under 
control of public transport companies (e.g. NFC used in 
iPhone’s can only be used through Apple Pay system). This 
means that new phones may have systems or settings that cause 
issues to mobile ticketing. He nce, public transport companies 
prefer means that they can c ontrol themselves (i.e. NFC or 
RFID (Radio-Frequency Identif ication) travel cards).  
3) Organisation domain. 
There were no challenges identified.   
4) Financial domain. 
Service cost . This challenge remains relevant.  
Summing up, analysis of business model of each type of 
mobile ticketing reveals a number of factors that have a 
negative effect on wider penetration of these services. In 
service domain, small assortment of tickets, exclusion of some 
groups of customers, and as a re sult, small market share, are 
the major challenges. A lack of travel statistics, need for 
infrastructure, security issu es, and discharging smartphone 
battery are the key issues of technology domain. The major 
challenges of finance domain are higher cost of mobile tickets 
and credit card fraud. And this leads to more detailed 
comparative analysis of key challenges across SMS, in-app, 
and BIBO ticketing services.  VI. COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

VII. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS  
Mobile ticketing services provide a number of advantages 
to different service stakeholders . Some examples of advantages 
for public transport companies are reduced amount of cash, convenient ticketing integrated w ith added value services. In 
the case of open ticketing system, mobile ticketing is much 
cheaper to maintain since there is no need for infrastructure. 
For customers, mobile ticketi ng means a fast and convenient 
way to get a ticket anytime avoiding queues to ticket vending 
machines.  
Despite mentioned advantages, the use of mobile ticketing 
services sets different challenges for public transport 
companies. We used the STOF model to analyse key business 
model elements and to reveal the major challenges associated 
with service, technology, orga nization, and finance, and 
affecting penetration of mobile ticketing. Based on empiric 
data, major factors that are preventing wider spread and use of 
mobile ticketing are related to service, technology, and 
finance. Organisation domain cau ses less problems, because 
there are clearly defined roles for actors in each type of mobile 
ticketing service. These act ors are contracted through 
procurement process.  
A. Business model related challenges 
With exception of Ruter and S kyss in Norway and mobile 
tickets used in Copenhagen in Denmark, the use of mobile 
ticketing in other countries does not exceed 10%. Ruter is 
extremely successful with mobile ticketing in Oslo. The share 
of mobile tickets is more th an 40%, and it is constantly 
growing. However, there still ar e customers who do not want to 
use mobile ticketing due to one or another reason. However, 
for public transport companies it is important to provide tickets 
to all types of passengers. This means that different ticketing 
systems and payment options mu st be offered in parallel. 
The major source of hesitation for customers is discharging 
phone battery. Practices to provide passengers with chargers or 
“power banks” failed, because phones need time to charge. 
Public transport companies are looking into new transport 
vehicles providing phone char ging opportunity on-board.  
From technology point, SMS and in-app ticketing do not 
provide travel statistics. Differe nt types of ticketing systems 
(open or barrier) put different challenges. The use of mobile 
ticketing is more advantageous in open system, while barrier 
system requires investment in  infrastructure for mobile 
ticketing. There are technological limits related to security and 
use of fraudulent payment cards in the apps. Additionally, it is 
more expensive to issue mobile tickets.  
There are a few competing technologies used at the same 
time. Due to challenges related to mobile ticketing, majority of 
public transport companies prefer their customers to use plastic 
travel cards. Cards are perceive d cheaper in use, having higher 
security, no fraud, and providing a better travel statistics. In the case of barrier ticketing system, needed infrastructure already 
is in place. Customers who have loaded money to the card are 
considered to be returning public transportation customers. 
Finally, bank transaction fee is lower when uploading bigger 
amount of money to pre-paid wallet on the travel card and later 
using these money for single trips,  than paying a fixed fee from 
each micro-transaction for single ticket (e.g. about 7–8% from 
10 or 30 SEK). With the help of price policy, customer choices 
are directed towards use of plastic cards. Majority of public 
transport companies offer disc ounts for tickets bought with the 
travel card. And these are the key issues negatively affecting 
penetration of mobile ticketing services. Development of new 
generation mobile ticketing soluti ons (e.g. BIBO) may help to 
overcome some technology challenges (i.e. lack of travel 
statistics) and reduce the cost of ticketing service infrastructure, 
however, a number of service, technology, and finance 
domains’ issues remain open.  
Mobile ticketing is one of use cases of mobile payments. 
The major obstacles negatively affecting spread of mobile 
payments in developed countri es are associated with: (1) 
consumer and merchant behavior  that are selecting between 
numerous payment instruments; (2) a range of competing 
technologies; (3) the mobile paym ent ecosystem that is stable 
and slowly changing, banks remain the main actors that handle 
transactions, and mobile payment services are linked to bank 
accounts or bank cards of users; and (4) regulation of financial 
domain sets certain requirements to payment service providers 
[35]. It is possible to track si milarities in mobile ticketing: 
public transport customers can  select different payment 
options: cash, payment card, m obile payment, invoices. There 
are different competing ticketi ng solutions running in parallel 
paper tickets, smart travel cards, mobile tickets (sometimes 
both SMS and in-app). Customer choice between these options 
might be directed according to  public transport company’s 
preferences. Mobile payment eco system mainly relies on card 
payments, and public transport companies face high transaction 
cost for single tickets. Finally, due to regulation, there can be a 
change of business network actors.  
B. Contribution and limitations 
The results of this study contribute to academic research. 
We applied the STOF framework in order to analyse existing 
business models [1], [2]. However, we specifically focused on 
key challenges. The STOF model allows structured approach in 
research and analysis of business models. Secondly, the 
research results extend knowledge about complexity of 
interrelated factors that affect the penetration scale of mobile 
ticketing.  
The study provides important gui delines for practitioners. 
The most significant research insight is that plastic smart card 
currently is a more preferred tic keting solution for a majority of 
public transport companies. This means that existing mobile 
ticketing services do not fully address all needs of public 
transport companies. Hence, the results might be useful for 
mobile ticketing service developers and providers giving the 
direction how the service could be  improved. At the same time, 
some challenges (e.g. high transaction cost, lack of unified 
standards, smartphone characteris tics) cannot be solved locally 
and require global agreements.
