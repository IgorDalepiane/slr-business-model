Abstract
Cloud-computing and Web 2.0 technologies have triggered a new wave of IT infrastructureadoption, which has been radically changing the way we live and work. In particular,Enterprise 2.0 and Enterprise Social Software (ESS) are getting more attention fromorganizations worldwide as they help staff to work and collaborate more efﬁciently. In this
paper, we introduce the case of a leading ﬁrm in China called Mingdao that provides an
ESS platform via the SaaS delivery model. Our teaching case gives insights into howMingdao experienced its platform’s early tortuous design and development, deﬁned itsbusiness and revenue model, and improved its platform’s various functions and features.Such organizational experiences not only reﬂect the opportunities and challenges of theESS market in China, but also present a new way of communication, collaboration, andsharing on the enterprise social network platform. This case also has implications forpractitioners for how cloud-computing and Web 2.0 technology can drive the
transformation of both enterprise and management as well as how the ESS platform
could help accelerate knowledge management activities across the organization.

2.0.’ Yet there’s a saying, ‘‘ Life is to continue facing many

Internet-based service business was huge, Mingdao still had
to confront mounting challenges.
The business intelligence reports during the ﬁrst few months
of 2015 were not that encouraging. There arose some questions
that may cause further headaches for the young, energetic CEO
of Mingdao. First, because a number of companies in Chinanow offer similar Enterprise 2.0 products, and even offer them

enterprises like Mingdao lack social and government support.
Though Mingdao already completed its A round ﬁnancing,how could it get more funding in the near future?
Moreover, Ren was not fully convinced of Mingdao’s
current approach to drawing more companies to try and
accept its platform. He wanted to know how to convincefree-trial customers of the excellent beneﬁts of upgrading to
being paid subscribers. Equally, he was more curious about
