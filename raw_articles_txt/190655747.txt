Abstract  – The interest towards solutions related to the 
management and analysis of disturbances in supply chains 
has increased, which has led to a pressure among technology and service providers to develop comprehensive business 
models to response customer needs. The aim of the study is 
to clarify the value elements of a business model for disturbance management and analysis in supply chains. Regarding to the elements of a business model, the paper 
concentrates on the analysis of customer segments, needs 
and demands related to the potential benefits for the customers. The research is done by the perspective of a solution provider developing a service entity to logistic 
intensive companies. The results of the study show that there 
are clear needs and benefits for the development of solutions for disturbance management and analysis. In addition, a SaaS based value proposition is stated for the new solution of 
disturbance management and analysis in supply chains. The 
value proposition is based on the determination of customer needs, solution approach, benefits and prevailing competition of the new service entity. 
 
Keywords
 –Business model, value proposition, customer 
needs, security management, disturbance management 
 
I.  INTRODUCTION 
 
Recently, the interest towards services and solutions 
for the management and analysis of disturbances in 
supply chains has increased in logistic-intensive companies. This means that product, technology and service providers are in a pressure for developing 
tempting business models which could be valuable for the 
businesses of logistic-intensive companies as potential clients. Development of the analysis and management tools, systems and software is not only the question of technological feasibility, but thorough understanding of 
customer’s business benefits is the key to success. Thus, 
there is a need for identifying and further analyzing the most essential customer needs, which may vary by different segments. Combining the analyses of client needs [1], demands and other market forces [2] with industry forces [3], key trends and macro-economic 
forces, supplements the perspectives of value proposition 
and other elements of the business model which dedicate to the value delivery [4]. 
The purpose of this study is to clarify the value 
elements of a business model for managing and analyzing disturbances in supply chains. Our research takes the 
perspective of a solution provider developing and offering 
the service entity to logistic-intensive firms. In this sense, the paper can be considered as an exploratory single case study [5] providing an addition to the current wide literature of business models and value creation research. The focus of the paper is on transforming the business 
model design from technology oriented model towards 
more comprehensive, customer-oriented model. Regarding the elements of a business model, the paper concentrates on the descriptions of value proposition and analysis of customer segments, needs and demands related to the potential benefits for the customer. 
The customer needs were collected by conducting 
semi-structured interviews in 14 companies, and the data was analyzed by using content analysis and the need interpretation table. Based on the collected and analyzed customer needs, the value proposition aspects can be 
described from the point of view of different segments, 
both cargo owners and logistic operators. The customer information related knowledge is analyzed together with the analyses of driving forces and general trends provided by the literature review, in order to make the value proposition feasible. Practically, the results of the study 
provide information about the elements of value for better 
understanding and communication of the benefits of disturbance management and analysis in the supply chain environment.  
II. SECURITY MANAGEMENT IN LOGISTIC 
PROCESSES 
 To achieve high level security in logistic processes is 
not an easy task, since supply chains often consist of wide networks of actors. Especially increased networking, internationalization of trade as well as expansion of the 
logistic industry have affected the importance of security 
management tools, methods and collaboration between stakeholders [6]. Security in logistics processes can be defined as protection from harm or damage, which intentional and unauthorized actions such as disturbances 
can cause to supply chains [7]. Although disturbance as a 
term has many definitions, e.g. disruption, incident, accident, failure, hazard, crisis [8], in general, disturbance can be seen as situations that effect negatively normal course of actions of the affected supply chain [9]. Disturbances can take place in many sources and when 
occurring in main operations of supply chains, 
disturbances can have significant operative and economic effects.

save costs and increase the effectiveness of logistic operations. Overall disturbance management in supply

management gives the company an opportunity to make 
choices that can have an effect on the occurrence of disturbances. In the predictive loss prevention, the analysis and monitoring actions of transport disturbances are in a significant role. 
 
III. THE ELEMENTS OF A BUSINESS MODEL  
 
Osterwalder and Pigneur [4] define the concept of a 
business model as “the rationale of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value”. They suggest that a 
business model can be described with nine basic building 
blocks, which cover the four main business areas: customers, offering, infrastructure, and financial viability. In the heart of the business model is the value proposition. In practice, it is a description of how to satisfy customer’s needs or how to solve customer’s problems with the help 
of products or services or a mixture of them [4]. 
The other elements of a business model describe how 
to deliver the value to the customer, while the value 
proposition describes the solution’s value itself from the customer perspective. The other elements may also add 
value to the customer and thus be strongly connected to 
the value proposition. Carlson and Wilmot [12], define the components of the value proposition as NABCs, where the letters refer to an important customer Needs , a 
product or service Approach , Benefits , and prevailing 
Competition . These components define the value 
proposition required for a customer. In addition, value propositions are also required for other stakeholders in the supply chains, e.g. for logistic operators. 
Security management or disturbance management in 
logistic networks are businesses where technology and technological systems often play quite a remarkable role. 
This is why it is essential from the business model point 
of view to describe the connection between the customer perspective and the value proposition including not only the technological aspects, but also the customer needs and benefits of the bundle of products and services that are valuable for the selected customer segments. The economic value of technologies may remain hidden until 
there is a suitable business model through which the value 
is delivered to the customers and revenues provided to the supplier [13]. 
The overall offerings portfolio of supplier’s products 
and services may be operated using the same business model. Alternatively, each product or service may have its 
own business model. The chosen business model may also 
be shared by multiple firms in order to provide a solution entity to the customer. The selection criteria of a business model are derived from the characteristics of the offered solution and the customers’ needs and requirements [14]. 
 
IV. RESEARCH PROCESS  
  The objective of the study is to find out the value elements of a new solution for managing and analyzing disturbances in supply chains. Our research takes the perspective of a solution provider, a company  Alfa,  which 
develops and offers a service entity to logistic-intensive companies. The service is new and the company does not yet have a specific business model related to the service, e.g. the cost structure and revenue streams are still under consideration. 
Company Alfa has been developing a new service 
entity for the management and analysis of transport disturbances. The new service is a database-based service solution that aims to provide a tool for companies to analyze and manage transport disturbances, and thus provide companies an opportunity to develop proactive 
risk management. The service allows a variety of models 
of co-operation with stakeholders (such as customers). The basic principle behind the service is to collect systemically information about customer’s transport disturbances to the information system. The service examines transportation disturbances and offers real-time 
monitoring, statistics and analysis of the disturbances. The 
solution is planned to work with Software as a Service (SaaS) principles (used and delivered through internet technology) and thus does not require special investments from a customer. Also other SaaS business characteristics 
(e.g. easy to deploy and deliver, online purchase and 
support, low price, high volume, common business processes, transferrable risk, and low budgetary expense) have an influence on the development of a business model [15]. 
The data of customer needs for the study was 
gathered by conducting semi-structured interviews in 14 logistic-intensive companies all around Finland. Together 16 informants were interviewed and 5 informants answered literally to the interview questions. The interviewed companies were chosen randomly among a relatively big number of potential users of the disturbance 
management solution in supply chains. The interviews 
were conducted in seven different industries. Table 1 presents the interviewed industries and the number of informants.  Proceedings of the 2013 IEEE IEEM

V. RESULTS 
 
A. Needs for managing and analyzing disturbances  
 
The results of the study show that there are clear 
needs for the development of solutions for disturbance 
management and analysis in supply chains. These needs can be distributed to address six different aspects of 
disturbance management and analysis. These were 
identified during the analysis as: Costs, Statistics, Reporting, Analysis, Systems and Organization. Table 2 presents the customer needs for the future solution related to these categories.  
According to our study, the customer needs related to 
the analysis and management of disturbances vary between different industries, because they have different characteristics and thus the vulnerability to disturbances varies. Also the effects that disturbances can cause to company´s operations in different industries vary significantly between industries. According to the interviews, three different types of customer segments for the new service for the management and analysis of 
transport disturbances can be identified; 1) Owners of the 
cargo (low value of transported goods), 2) Owners of the cargo (high value of transported goods) and 3) logistic operators.  
Our results show that among owners of the cargo, the 
particular interest towards the transport disturbance 
management and analysis focuses on perceiving the 
overall picture of transport disturbances and their effects on company’s operations (e.g. what kind of transport problems there are and what the related costs are?).

B. Benefits of the management and analysis of disturbances 
 
The results of the study indicate that there are clear 
benefits that the management and analysis of disturbances 
bring to companies. The transparent systems for 
disturbance management and analysis could e.g. enhance the reputation of a company,  make operations more fluent, increase quality, create customer satisfaction, help to avoid disappointed customers, create competitive 
advantage, and enable information to be available anytime 
and for those who need it. In general, during the analysis five clearly distinguishable themes related to benefits of the management and analysis on disturbances were identified. These are 1) Follow- up costs, 2) Efficiency, 3) 
Reporting, 4) Statistics and 5) Reputation, which are 
depicted in Figure 1. 
 One of the possibilities of the management and analysis of disturbances is the follow-up of small costs and in general the follow up of costs caused by disturbances. In particular, the transport disturbances can cause direct but also indirect costs, which are often hard 
to manage. The management and analysis of these costs 
could offer companies a way to point out the problems and focus on operational efficiency. Increased operational efficiency and costs saving are seen both as benefits and also as prerequisites for the management system in all customer segments. According to our results, this is one of the keys, when designing the value proposition for the 
new solution for disturbance management and analysis. 
 When occurring, transport disturbances can have a significant effect to the operations of customers as well as cargo owners or logistic operators. One of the main benefits that the disturbance management of transportations brings is the avoidance of disappointed 
customers. Ways to do that can be, among other things, 
sticking to the causes of disturbances and transparent reporting of these disturbances.  The benefits to the different customers segments have some differences due to the different contracts between actors in supply chains such as transport agreements and 
insurances. This affects the value proposition of the 
designed business model. Especially for the owners of a cargo, the disturbance reports and analysis could offer a tool to be used in the evaluations of suppliers and logistic operators. Therefore, systems could help in motivating logistic operators to minimize transportation disturbances 
and thus to reduce the costs for the cargo owners.
