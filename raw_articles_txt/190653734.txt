Abstract — In the digital age, the concept of monetization has 
undergone significant transformations, particularly within the 
mobile app industry. For mobile app developers, the 
identification of an optimal monetization strategy is crucial for 
achieving success. Thi s study aims to thoroughly investigate 
monetization strategies, trends, challenges, and best practices in 
mobile application development. The ultimate goal is to assist 
firms in selecting the most suitable revenue stream while 
ensuring the delivery of high -quality applications to users. To 
accomplish this, the author conducted a comprehensive 
systematic literature review encompassing a wide range of 
topics, including monetization types such as freemium, in -app 
purchases, and in -app advertising, as well as t he potential 
challenges and opportunities associated with each strategy. The 
findings reveal that among the three types of monetization 
strategies —free, premium, and freemium —the latter proves to 
be the most advantageous. However, the successful 
implementa tion of freemium requires careful consideration of 
various factors. To address this, the author proposed a diagram 
consisting of six levels of monetization strategies. This diagram 
serves as a practical tool that enhances firms’ awareness and 
enables them to make informed decisions when selecting the 
appropriate strategy for their mobile applications.  
Keywords — app monetization ; freemium ; mobile application;  
mobile marketing  
I. INTRODUCTION  
 Mobile applications have grown significantly in popularity 
over the past three decades and have integrated themselves 
significantly into daily life [21]. They are made up of a 
collection of apps created especially for mobile devices [20]. 
Mobile devices have a number of significant benefits, 
including its portability, which makes it easy for users to 
manage and carry the device wherever they go. Mobile apps 
stand out for their usability, user -friendliness, cost, and 
compatibility with a variety of mobile phones, including entry -
level and budget -friendly models [20].  
 According to a Grand View Research analysis, the 
worldwide mobile market is expected to increase at a 13.8% 
compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between 2023 and 
2030, reaching a value of USD 206.85 billion in 2022. 
According to this growth projection, the mobile market might 
generate USD 567.19 billion in revenue by 2030. Such trends present lucrative opportunities for firms to strategically 
position their businesses and capitalize on the potential for 
substantial revenue generation [22].  
 The development of a suitable monetization strategy is 
crucial for businesses, although many firms may 
underestimate its complexity. Take the case of Vine, a once 
popular social media platform, which is said to have failed in 
part due to its lack of a pro per monetization plan. By 2014, 
Vine had 200 million users and was an extremely well -liked 
app that let users make and share 6 -second videos. According 
to a Failory post, Vine's failure as a hyper -growth network 
effect business was due to its reluctance to  implement a 
suitable revenue solution following the platform's explosive 
user growth. This includes Vine's failure to pursue appropriate 
sponsorship or advertising opportunities, even after Twitter 
purchased the business [12].  
 Retention is another crucial aspect to consider when deter - 
mining a monetization strategy. As pointed out by Bothma, a 
mere 3% of users continue using an application after 30 days 
[6]. Consequently, it becomes vital to pay attention to each 
phase of the user’s journey, starting from installati on, during 
active usage, and even addressing the potential for 
uninstallation. Understanding and optimizing these stages are 
essential for developing an effective monetization strategy.  
 In this paper, the author provides an in -depth exploration 
of the various monetization strategies prevalent in the mobile 
market. This analysis is conducted through an investigation of 
marketing and technology journals published within the last 
five years . The findings reveal several commonly employed 
strategies in the mobile market. Notably, in -app purchases, 
advertising, and freemium models have emerged as the most 
prevalent and favored options in today’s market.  Furthermore, 
a significant literature gap  has been identified regarding the 
clarity of which monetization strategy should be employed, 
especially in the context of general applications where 
multiple strategies could be applicable. In this paper, a 
comprehensive diagram is proposed to provide a b etter 
understanding of the best practices for selecting the most 
appropriate mobile application monetization strategy.

C. Literature Review  
 RQ1: What types of monetization strategies exist in the  
mobile  market?  
In recent years, studies have traditionally classified mobile 
app monetization strategies into two distinct categories: free 
and premium. However, there exists a transition area that 
exhibits a wide range of revenue generation approaches, 
commonly referred  to as “freemium” [23]. The phrase 
"freemium" refers to two different versions of a product: a free 
version with restricted functionality and a complete version 
that costs money but has more features and capabilities. It is a 
mix of the terms “free” and “premium ” [24]. This business 
model has gained significant traction and prevalence, with a 
majority of the literature in this field referencing the freemium 
strategy as their primary focus of study.  Among users, free 
applications are highly favored, primarily due to their zero -
cost nature. However, for firms, sustaining an application 
solely on a free monetization strategy can pose challenges, 
although it is not entirely impossible. Previous studies h ave 
highlighted that the concept of “free” does not imply a lack o f 
exchange. In one paper, Salehudin quotes Milton Friedman, 
stating that “There’s no such thing as a free lunch” [19]. It has 
been found that even with a seemingly “free” monetization 
strategy, firms can still derive benefits from users’ personal 
data [7].  The same paper also emphasizes that while users may

 The efficacy of premium applications in attracting and 
retaining users has been established through prior research. 
Appel et al. introduced the concept of “stickiness” as a 
measure of user engagement and revenue generation in the 
context of premium applica tions [11]. By focusing on the 
development of premium applications, firms can enhance user 
stickiness, which positively impacts their business 
performance. In addition to the aforementioned strategies, 
implementing one -time purchases and subscription -based 
models are common practices within the premium app market. 
Furthermore, offering trial versions of premium apps has been 
recognized as an effective approach to stimulate sales and 
attract potential customers [11]. These insights have been 
derived from th e literature review conducted in this study, 
underscoring the significance of appropriate treatment and 
development strategies in optimizing the performance of 
premium applications.  
 The freemium monetization strategy has emerged as a 
prominent and widely studied topic. Scholars have recognized 
it as a “de facto business model,” wherein firms leverage user 
demand through inconvenience [1]. Freemium offers 
extensive benefits and flexib ility, enabling firms to gradually 
increase sales by adopting a specific monetization approach. 
A key component of the freemium strategy is providing 
substantial benefits to users who are willing to pay for 
enhanced treatment and privileges [10]. This can include 
leveraging network effects, where users’ purchasing decisions 
are influenced by the actions and recommendations of their 
own network [15], [24]. Developers employ various practices 
to implement freemium strategies, with in -app purchases 
(IAP) and i n-app advertising being the primary approaches 
used [4], [26], [27].  
 In addition to the various monetization strategies discussed 
above, many firms also employ a hybrid marketing approach. 
This approach involves offering both a free or sample version 
of an app, along with a premium version. When implemented 
effectively, th e presence of a “damaged goods” app, 
characterized by the inclusion of ads or blocked features, can incentivize users to purchase the premium version [23]. Users 
often download the sample version to experience a preview of 
the premium application. The succ ess of this implementation 
is measured by the “conversion rate” [3], which will be further 
examined in the analysis of RQ 2 in this study.

 RQ2: What are the key factors influencing the success of  
mobile  app monetization  strategies?  
 It is important to acknowledge that there is no one -size- 
fits-all solution for the success of monetization strategies. The 
effectiveness of these strategies varies based on the type and 
category of the application, as emphasized by Cecere et al. [7]. 
Each firm must carefully evaluate and tailor their monetization 
approach to align with their specific application and business 
goals.  
 The presence of free users in the freemium landscape is 
crucial as it contributes to the overall success of the strategy. 
It provides a sense of “pride” for paying users, as mentioned 
in a study [18]. Additionally, the presence of asymmetry in 
network eff ects plays a significant role in determining the 
success of freemium applications, as highlighted in another 
study [4], [10].  
 Furthermore, the concept of freemium highlights the 
importance of distinction between the baseline quality of the 
product for free and paying users. It is crucial for firms to 
define the maximum features available for free users and the 
minimum features o ffered to premium users, as this distinction 
fundamentally plays into the network effects of the strategy. If 
a firm fails to properly define these boundaries, the freemium 
strategy may not serve as the most effective monetization 
strategy [4].  
 Li offers yet another viewpoint on the difficulties of 
putting a freemium strategy into practice. According to Li, 
adopting and concentrating on freemium with in -app 
purchases can produce the greatest results if the basic service 
quality is too low and the  premium service quality is too high. 
On the other hand, it is preferable to adopt and concentrate on 
the freemium with in -app advertising approach if the basic 
service quality is too high and the premium service quality is 
too poor.  In these cases, the f reemium strategy provides a 
meaningful deal for customers, encouraging them to continue 
paying for additional features or removing ads. However, if 
the value proposition is not well -balanced between the basic 
and premium services, it can result in users no longer paying 
or even refraining from purchasing in the first place, as there 
is no significant incentive for them to do so [13] .

 In this research, the author came across three studies that 
investigate the significance of "uncertainty" in the freemium 
strategy. These studies suggest that when there is a surprise in 
the quality of a freemium application, it can lead to higher 
sales. This phenomenon occurs because when customers 
underestimate the significance of an app initially and 
subsequently discover that its v alue is higher than expected, 
such as through a trial period or a sample app, they are more 
inclined to generate additional revenue within the freemium 
model. This is attributed to the concept of "upgrade," where 
users voluntarily seek to access enhanced f eatures or benefits 
by making a financial contribution. The studies referenced as 
sources provide further insights into this topic [3], [9], [11].  
 A literature indicates that while freemium can be an 
attractive strategy, it cannot solely be justified based on 
acquiring a large user base. Too many free users can harm 
businesses and reduce revenue. When the availability of free 
services threatens profitability, this process is known as the 
“cannibalization ” of paying clients. The results of this study 
demonstrate how providing free goods or services may boost 
sales but reduce profitability. It is crucial to remember that 
while providing a free product may enable a market leader to 
obtain a competitive advantage by accumulating a sizable user 
base, this advantage does not ensure increasing profitability. 
In reality, it may dramatically impair the profitability of 
followers without ensuring larger profits  for the market leader 
[17].  
 Measuring the effectiveness of a freemium model can be 
done through time retention, which serves as a strong indicator 
of monetization success. Time retention reflects how long 
users engage with the app over time, and it is considered a 
significant predic tor of revenue generation [2]. However, it's 
important to note that time retention may not be applicable in 
all cases. For instance, in the case of TikTok, time retention is 
not a crucial factor because its main source of income comes 
from advertising reve nue and in -app purchases rather than 
watch time alone [2], [ 28]. Therefore, while time retention can 
be a valuable metric for assessing the success of a freemium 
model, it is not universally applicable and must be considered 
in the context of each specific app and its monetization 
strategy.  
 In the instance of user retention, the author locates a 
research that explains three key points: installation, user 
retention, and removal. App adoption is influenced during 
installation by elements including online comfort, the 
registration procedure, eas e of trust, and clear instructions. 
User comfort, usability, aesthetics, customer service, 
credibility, storage, performance, and simplicity of adoption 
all play a significant part in the user retention phase. However, 
several elements —such as time consum ption, unmet 
expectations, app size, reputation, social impact, worries about 
personal data, and perceived usefulness —can cause an app to 
be uninstalled [2].  
 The authors identify two main ideas of digital marketing 
from Stochhi. Firstly, apps can gain a competitive edge by 
adopting a digital marketing orientation, utilizing personalized 
consumer insights and embracing technological advancements 
to enhance thei r value proposition. Secondly, creating positive 
customer experiences and journeys is essential, achieved by 
emphasizing the app's value as a channel for accessing 
products and services and enabling seamless interactions [29].   After conducting a comprehensive review of the existing 
literature, the author identified a significant gap in the research 
regarding the clarity of which monetization strategy should be 
employed, particularly in the context of general applications 
where multiple strategies could be applicable. Surprisingly, 
the author found a lack of literature that provides a 
comprehensive understanding of the underlying principles and 
considerations for choosing each monetization strategy, while 
also acknowledging and m anaging the associated risks. Thus, 
the author aimed to address these critical issues through this 
study.

focuses on identifying the best monetization strategy for firms 
to implement. The authors have proposed a diagram in Fig . 2

diagram serves as a guide for firms to choose the most suitable 
monetization strategy for their specific needs and goals.  
In Fig . 2, the authors have illustrated six levels of 
monetization strategies. These levels represent varying 
degrees of strategical importance, ranging from 5, where users 
are required to fully pay for the application, to 0, indicating 
that users do not need to sp end any money as the application 
is completely free. The details of the diagram are presented in

 It is important to note that the diagram in Figure 2 is not 
intended to depict the exact amount of revenue a firm will 
generate by implementing a higher level. Instead, its purpose 
is to provide an illustrative approach that showcases the 
“degree of paying. ” This concept is meant to demonstrate the 
varying levels or degrees to which customers may engage in 
paying for the implemented features or services.

B. On Optimizing Monetization Strategy  
To create optimal pricing, firms need to assess each 
feature the application offers. The author suggests 
considering aspects such as “stickiness” and “uncertainty” 
[11]. These features may be seen in the trial version with 
advertisements, where the busines s earns money through 
advertisements while the trial is in effect. Higher uncertainty 
might draw users who might not otherwise download the app 
because they do so when they think the prospective 
advantages outweigh the early expenses of trying it out [10],  
[11].  
Customer retention is a different optimization factor that 
businesses should take into account. Enhancing user comfort, 
ease of use, aesthetics, customer service, trust, storage, 
performance, and simplicity of adoption can help the 
organization increase re tention [6]. A shorter contract length 
is also fundamentally linked to a lower termination rate [5].

In this study, the authors reviewed the literature for the 
last five years to find common practices in free, premium, and 
freemium mobile monetization methods. The findings 
highlight an absence in the research as there are currently no  
studies that thoroughly describe and compare each 
monetization method.  To bridge this gap, the authors propose 
a six-level monetization strategy diagram, ranging from level 
5 to 0. These tiers are, in order, free, sample & premium (two 
versions of the program), in -app adverti sing, in -app 
purchases, semi -premium, premium, and in -app advertising. 
The authors examine market data from nine top -20 
applications to verify the usefulness of the suggested diagram. 
The accompanying evidence enhances the suggested strategy 
diagram's beli evability. Freemium is also proven to be the 
best technique to apply in the mobile industry since it is 
flexible and has the ability to attract a larger user base and 
generate more income.  
This research makes a significant contribution to mobile 
marketing by offering a descriptive methodology and 
suggesting a monetization diagram to identify various 
monetization options, which might be a useful tool for 
businesses as they pick their monetiza tion approach while 
taking into account the accompanying difficulties.  Future 
research should focus on several mobile application 
categories, including gaming, social networking, retail and e -
commerce, music and entertainment, among others. By doing 
this, it will be possible to gain a deeper insight of how 
different app categories monetize their content.  REFERENCES
