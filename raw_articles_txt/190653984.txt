Abstract —A specialized  information  system  (SIS)  is a type of 
information  system  that is designed  to support  a specific  
business  or organization.  SISs  are typically  used  to manage  
large  amounts  of data.  A software  platform  is a kind  of software  
that creates  value  through  a synergy,  by surrounding  itself  with  
an ecosystem  of external  connections,  data,  and processes.  The 
goal of platform  thinking  is to build  a software  platform  that is 
a foundation  for a new ecosystem.  In this paper,  we distill  the 
best practice  of building  a succe ssful  software  platform by 
learning  from  the history  of Salesforce,  SAP,  and Amazon.  We 
also offer a set of guidelines  to help SISs  transform  into software  
platforms.  However,  we describe  that a software  platform  is not 
a superior  software  architecture,  but rather  a new business  
model .  
Keywords— specialized information system, software platform, 
platform economy, data layer, semantic layer, presentation layer, 
customizability, business requirements  
INTRODUCTION  
Traditional specialized  software  systems  are architected  to 
solve specific  business problems. For example,  a banking 
systems’s  core application  is designed to facilitate  banking 
transactions,  while  another system  focuses  on managing  cus-
tomer  information.  In many  cases,  these  applications are not 
integrated.  What  happens if a business needs  to integrate  
them?  A common solution is to buy an expensive integration 
platform that connects  the specialized  software  systems,  
thereby  allow -ing them  to share  information. This solution 
can work  well if the platform is compatible  with the 
underlying software  applications and is configured correctly.  
However,  it is an expensive solution that requires  specialized  
skills  to implement  and maintain  [1-5]. Therefore,  what  we 
see nowadays, is businesses  are choosing platform -based  
SISs.  A software  platform  has the following benefits:   
1) All-in-one solution: platforms  provide an all-in-one so-
lution  for businesses.  Unlike specialized  software,  plat-
forms  don’t need  to be integrated  with other software;  
2) Reduced  costs:  platfor ms are often  less expensive than 
buying and integrating  several  specialized  software  
applications;  
3) Ease  of use: platforms  are designed to be user-friendly 
and easy to use;  
4) Flexibility:  platforms are flexible  and can be 
customized  to meet  the specific  needs  of a business;   
5) Scalability:  platforms  can be easily  scaled  to 
accommodate  the growing needs  of a business.    
We are going to focus on the benefits  of the flexibility  of 
software  platforms.  Whenever  a business has a new 
requirement  – they no longer need  to ask a software  vendor 
to implement  a new feature.  By utilizing  the flexibility  of a 
software  platform,  they can implement  a tiny component and 
plug it into the ecosystem.  There  are a few key benefits  to 
this:  
1) Speed  to market:  by being able to quickly implement  
new features,  businesses  can be much  more agile  and 
responsive to market  demands;  
2) Cost savings: developing new features  in-house can be 
much  cheaper  than paying for a vendor to do it;  
3) Increased  control: by having more  control over the soft-
ware,  businesses  can ensure  that it meets  their specific  
needs.   
A platform ecosystem  is built around a platform.  The 
ecosystem  can be defined  as a group of organizations that are 
connected  to the platform through APIs  or other means.  The 
ecosystem  is made  up of three  types of organizations: 
platform providers, app developers, and users.  Platform  
providers are the organizations that build  and operate  the 
platform.  App developers are the organizations that build 
apps that run on the pla tform.  Users  are the individuals or 
organizations that use the apps that run on the platform.   
The concept  of the platform  economy has been  around for 
centuries.  The term itself  was first coined  in the 17th century, 
when  economists realized  that certain  industries, such as 
shipping and finance,  depended on a network of connected  
businesses,  or a ”platform.”  In the 19th century,  the railroads  
and telegraph  expanded the platform economy by creating  
new markets  and new ways  of doing business.  In the 20th 
centu ry, the automobile and telephone created  even  more 
opportunities for businesses  to interact  with one another. And 
in the 21st century, the Internet  has become  the ultimate  
platform economy, connecting people and businesses  around 
the world.   
The key to success for a platform is to build an ecosystem  
around the platform.  The purpose of the ecosystem  is to 
create  a virtuous circle  that benefits  all three  types of 
organizations: platform providers, app developers, and users  
[6-10]. The virtuous circle  is created  when  the app developers 
build apps that the users  want  to use. The users  use the apps 
and generate  data.

II. RELATED  WORK 
The software platform consists of the following layers: 
data, processing, and presentation. So far, there is no 
comprehensive research that covers all of the components at 
once. There is some work about API-based platforms and 
how they are extensible [ 11-14 ]. But in 2022 many are 
arguing that the software developer ’s job boils down to 
integrating poorly documented APIs. We doubt that making 
an extensible API is enough to build a software platform.  
There are studies on the success of Salesforce [ 15,16 ] 
which are pretty dated, but helpfully illustrate how flexible a 
data layer of a software platform is.  
A work by Hubspot [ 17-20 ] lays out a foundation of a good 
distinction between a software platform versus an enclosed 
system. We borrowed some ideas on the reasoning when 
choosing a platform versus an SIS.  
We found one of the most inspiring works that talks about 
the best practices of software platforms was  Stevey’ s Google 
Platforms Rant [ 16]. Stevey talks about the design principles 
for  software  platforms.  According  to  him,  the  goal  of  a  
software  platform  should  be  to  reduce  the  cost  of  building  
applications.  They  say  that  the  platform  should  hide  
complexity  and  make  it  easy  to  utilize  the  underlying  
functionality.  The  work  says  the  entire  success  of  Amazon  
was b/c of the right way to build teams. They built a platform 
by forcing each team to use a product of another team, instead 
of  sharing  common  services.  They  call  this  approach  the  2 
pizza rule.  
III. METHOD  
In this paper, we conducted a qualitative case study based 
on secondary data sources. We collected data from 
Salesforce, SAP, and Amazon. We also collected their annual 
reports, financial statements, and other public documents. We 
used a variety of sources, including press releases, news 
articles, blog posts, and academic papers. We analyzed the 
data using a variety of methods, including content analysis, 
document analysis, and comparative analysis. Content 
analysis of these data sources helps us synthesize the main 
characteristics of these companies and their software 
platforms. Document analysis helps us understand these 
companies' business models. Comparative analysis helps us 
compare and contrast the characteristics of these companies 
and their software platforms.   
We also conducted interviews with experts in the field, 
including software developers, software architects, and 
software platform experts.  
 
We began by developing a list of criteria for a successful 
software platform. We then analyzed the data to identify the 
commonalities among the three companies. We also 
identified the key success factors for each company. 
Salesforce: focus on the customer, build an ecosystem, 
innovate. SAP: big data and analytics, establish a partner 
ecosystem, focus on the customer. Amazon: big data and 
analytics, build a platform, innovate.  Results from interviews with experts who recently 
transitioned their specialized information system into a 
software platform showed that:   
1) Platform thinking mindset is key to enable the business 
transformation;  
2) The organizational culture needs to be adapted to be 
able to embrace the platform thinking mindset;  
3) A common set of architecture principles is critical to 
align different teams within the organization;  
4) A platform team that is responsible for enabling the 
platform business model is essential;  
An ecosystem of partners and developers is necessary for 
the success of the platform.  
FINDINGS  
The most common software platform used for building SIS 
is the web platform. The web platform consists of a web 
server, a web application server, and a web browser. The web 
server is responsible for hosting the web application and 
serving it to the web browser. The web application server is 
responsible for running the business logic of the web 
application. The web browser is responsible for rendering the 
UI of the web application. The business logic is extended 
with a semantic layer.  
We will present our findings as they are relevant to the 
generic knowledge management system. Think of it as a place 
where people store, analyze, and access content. An example 
of a traditional approach of building a KMS described by S. 
A. Hakim [ 21-25 ] forces you to build a system that is not 
extensible from the outside by locking the application 
interface contracts to a specific consumer. On the other hand, 
using an SIS as a software platform like Salesforce enables 
us to build custom applications [ 26-28 ]. 
Building a traditional SIS is good when you have no plans 
of extending it in the future. You can build it to meet your 
specific needs, and if you need to add features later, you can 
do so as well. You can also customize the SIS as you wish, 
so that it meets your specific needs.  
However, if you plan on expanding your SIS in the future, 
then it is a good idea to build a software platform instead. 
This is because a software platform is much easier to extend 
and maintain than a traditional SIS. All of this time, effort, 
and money that needs to be put into building an SIS from 
scratch can be put towards building other software platforms 
When building a software platform, one no longer needs to 
worry about losing customers due to a lack of features or lack 
of customizability.  
Next, we ’re going to inspect each module of the SIS and 
what steps to make to transform your SIS into a software 
platform.  
Data Layer  
The  data  layer  is  the  lowest  level  of  the  software  
architecture. It is responsible for storing and retrieving data. A 
software platform must have a data layer that is scalable and 
redundant. A software platform must also have a data layer 
that can be shared by multiple applications. 
Each of the examined companies has a different approach 
to the data layer. Salesforce uses a relational database to store 
data. SAP uses an in-memory database to store data. Amazon 
uses a NoSQL database to store data.

One starts by building a data layer, which defines the data 
model that will be used by the entire platform. They then 
move on to building an application layer, which defines the 
user interface and allows applications to be built on top of the 
platform. After that, they can move on to creating an interface 
layer, which allows for business logic to be implemented on 
top of the platform. Finally, they can move on to creating a 
plugin system, which allows for plugins to be developed and 
installed on top of the platform.  
Each  of  the  three  companies  has  some  sort  of  search  
functionality which we use to illustrate the point. In a scenario 
of building a federated search engine, an SIS author needs to 
write  code  to  process  files  from  various  sources,  like  a  
filesystem, Sharepoint, Google Drive, etc. Each source has a 
different data schema and needs to be handled accordingly: 
parsing files, extracting text from them, and unifying different 
property  names.  All  of  that  requires  a  developer ’s  effort.  
Imagine each system has a filename stored under a different 
property: title , subject , name , filename . But in the 
SIS database, the name of the file has to be strictly under one 
property  name  title .  As  a  new  source  is  added,  more  
development  is  required  to  account  for  that.  The  effort  of  
maintaining and supporting a federated search engine grows 
with each new source. And what happens when a customer  
wants to add a new source? They will have to wait for the next 
software release. The SIS developers will have to add the new 
source to the code and release a new version as depicted in the 
“Fig. 1”. 
 
Fig. 1. Traditional SIS way of data ingestion  
In order to make the federated search engine more flexible 
and easier to maintain, the SIS author could use a data 
processing pipeline. A data processing pipeline is a series of  
steps  that  data  goes through  in  order  to  be  transformed  
from one format to another. In this case, the data processing 
pipeline would take in files from various sources, parse them, 
extract  the text, and unify  the different  property names. By  
using a data processing pipeline, the SIS author can easily add 
new sources to the federated search engine without having to 
write  new  code.  The  data  processing  pipeline  can  be  
configured to handle new sources automatically. This makes 
the  federated  search  engine  more  flexible  and  easier  to  
maintain, as depicted in  “Fig. 2”. This has the added effect of 
reducing the number of God classes [ 29] that might be hiding 
in your software.  
Fig. 2. SIS Platform way of Data Ingestion 
 
By adding a data pipeline for data ingestion, and exposing 
only a unified interface, the SIS benefits from freed 
engineering resources, as authors no longer need to spend 
time writing custom connectors to different sources.   
B. Semantic Layer  
 
In the last few years, the term semantic layer has become a 
common buzzword in the information systems field. It refers 
to a conceptual level that is on top of the physical level 
(database structure) and underneath the presentation level 
(application interface). In other words, it is an abstraction 
layer that goes between the data store and data consumers to 
allow them to work with the data in terms of a comprehensive 
business model.  
For the application developer, the semantic layer provides 
a well-defined interface to the database. This means that the 
developer can focus on the business logic of the application 
without having to worry about the details of the database 
structure. For the user, the semantic layer provides a 
consistent view of the data. This is because the semantic layer 
is designed to present the data in terms of the business model, 
rather than the underlying database structure.  
The idea is to expose the semantic layer to external devel-
opers so they can build custom integrations to enrich it with 
custom taxonomies, ontologies, and other semantic elements. 
The implementation of the semantic layer should be done in 
a way that minimizes the amount of code that needs to be 
written and maximizes the flexibility of the system. The 
semantic layer should be implemented as a set of software 
components that can be plugged into the system to provide 
the desired functionality.  
For example, a component could be used to generate a 
taxonomy for a specific domain, such as the medical domain. 
This component could be used to generate a taxonomy for the 
domain that is specific to the needs of the medical domain. 
The taxonomy could be used to represent the relationships 
between different concepts in the domain. Instead of baking 
in the code that derives the taxonomies into an SIS, authors 
should utilize the benefits of the platform, and allow exter-
nal parties to provide taxonomies from outside. A common 
scenario observed is storing the taxonomy for the content in 
an external system where it ’s defined by humans [ 30]. Back

Presentation Layer  
 
A software platform can also be used to build a variety of 
interfaces. With a traditional SIS, one needs to create a 
separate user interface for each feature that they want to 
implement. With a software platform, one can create an 
interface that handles the user interface for a variety of 
different features. This means that one does not have to write 
a separate user interface for each feature that they want to 
implement. A pioneer and the leader in this space is SAP. 
SAP ’s ability to customize UI ’s for specific customers have 
been critical to SAP ’s success3. The UI layer is also the layer 
that contains all of the buttons, menus, and dialog boxes. In 
order to change the UI, one must have access to the source 
code. In some cases, this may mean that the user interface 
must be re-written from scratch. On the other hand, SAP 
provides a wide range of UI tools that allow one to customize 
the UI without having to re-write it from scratch [ 32].  
An SIS that wants to adopt a software platform way to 
build a presentation layer that is easy to extend must follow 
the following architectural patterns:  
1) The ability to register UI elements to appear in different 
parts of the application via a plugin architecture: 
content, location, triggers, events; 
2) Force modules to speak with each other via a message 
bus rather than accessing code directly; 
3) Dogfooding4: adopt the plugin architecture for their 
own features; 
4) Maintain a developer ’s documentation for third  parties. 
In order to transform into a true software platform, one has 
to build the presentation layer of an SIS in such a way, that the 
code SIS authors write, should not have more privilege than 
the code external parties could write. This means interacting 
with a semantic layer rather than accessing the database. This 
means styling the UI via plugin parameters rather than hard-
coding style attributes in the common stylesheet. This means 
modularizing  the  code  so  that  different  features  can  be  
implemented as different plugins and turned on or off without affecting other features. The final result should be an SIS that 
is easy to modify and extend without having to understand the 
entire codebase. 
IV. FUTURE  WORK 
The guidelines described in this paper are not exhaustive, 
and there is much more work to be done in order to build a 
software platform from an SIS. In particular, we have not 
addressed the issue of how to monetize a software platform. 
This is an important question that must be addressed in order 
to make a platform successful. In addition, we have not 
addressed the issue of how to build a community around a 
platform. More work needs to be done to gather metrics to 
assess the success of platforms quantitatively.  
Furthermore, when implementing any organizational 
change, one should assess the cost of change and the potential 
for resistance to change which is the scope for future research 
to highlight when it makes sense to keep the SIS operational 
for the business and when a full transformation into a 
platform may be the best option.  
 
VI.  CONCLUSION  
SISs can provide great value to organizations, build they 
are not built as platforms from scratch when using agile 
methods [ 33]. A software platform is a type of software that 
creates value by surrounding itself with an ecosystem of 
external connections, data, and processes. The goal of 
platform thinking is to build a software platform that is a 
foundation for a new ecosystem. In this paper, we have 
described how SISs can be transformed into software 
platforms.  
We examined examples of other successful platforms that 
started as specialized information systems and transformed 
into well-known software platforms like SAP, Salesforce, 
Amazon to distill the best practice:   
1) Combinatorial Innovation: a platform enables 
combinatorial innovation by allowing third parties to 
build on top of it. This is done by providing an open 
and well-documented API that third parties can use to 
develop their own products and services; 
2) Ecosystem Enablers: A platform surrounds itself with 
an ecosystem of partners, developer tools, and other 
services that make it easy for third parties to develop 
products and services that work with the platform; 
3) Data Democratization: A platform democratizes access 
to data by making it easy for third parties to access and 
use data from the platform. This is done by providing 
an open and well-documented API that third parties can 
use to access data from the platform.  
A platform is not a superior software architecture, but 
rather a new business model. SISs should only be built as 
platforms if the organization wants to enable an ecosystem of 
innovation around the SIS. This frees up the development 
resources of the organization to focus on other priorities. 
Building an ecosystem also allows the organization to tap into 
the collective intelligence of the ecosystem to build new 
features and services.  
Lastly, given Conway ’s Law [ 34], we believe that building 
an SIS as a platform has a positive effect on the organizational 
structure  which  will  result  in  an  organization  that  is  more  
modular and can adapt more easily to change.
