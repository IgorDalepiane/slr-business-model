ABSTRACT
In an increasingly digital world, technology-based start-ups face 
challenges which are of unique nature and hence cannot be 
fathomed by organizations belonging to another genre. The 
case study focuses on a series of strategic and business model 
redesigning challenges faced by Ashutosh Chouksey, Executive 
Vice President – Expansion and Strategic Operations, while co- 
leading a technology-based customer engagement and digital 
signage solutions start-up, MintM. MintM has a business to 
business (B2B) based model that relies on its own proprietary 
software to display targeted audio-visual content in stores and 
supermarkets, offering the unique value proposition of simulta -
neous last mile advertising connectivity and customer analysis. 
The core product of MintM requires regular upkeep creating 
a conflict: a chance of global outreach versus operational has-
sles hindering its growth trajectory. The case is an appropriate 
platform for demonstrating information technology-based busi -
ness model management dilemma in a fast-changing digital 
milieu, whether to improvise on organizational structure or 
product particulars. The case also presents an opportunity to 
discuss operational challenges hindering expansion plans and 
how to strategize and redesign business model to overcome the 
same and cater to clientele in a niche nascent market while still 
maintaining a lean team.KEYWORDS

Prologue
It was a freezing winter night in India, sometime around the Christmas of 
2018. Ashutosh Chouksey, the executive vice president of a promising tech-
nology-based customer engagement start-up MintM, stayed back in his office 
till the wee hours, engrossed in deep thoughts concerning some urgent 
decisions that needed to be made to ensure the sustenance of his venture. 
The signature product of MintM, proprietary software to display targeted 
audio-visual content based on customer demographics in supermarkets, 
offered a unique value proposition, albeit with numerous operational and 
strategic challenges that resolutely hindered its growth trajectory. Although 
it was still the growth phase of MintM, it was becoming difficult to maintain 
healthy expansion, as the current business model was heavy on operations and 
the lean team at MintM was not equipped to cater to the demanding needs of 
the current model. To Ashutosh, this seemed like the enormous efforts of the 
initial years were going down the drain as growth started dwindling. He had to 
do something, and soon.
MintM was one-of-a-kind, both in terms of product type and market 
segment. It offered customer engagement and digital signage solutions along 
with analytics in a B2B set up. There were no competitors in the pertinent 
segment offering similar services in India. MintM was not only a B2B but also JOURNAL OF INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY CASE AND APPLICATION RESEARCH

Technology and usage of MintM
MintM’s cloud based smart signage solution product is termed ‘Magnet.’ On 
any android platform, Magnet can display relevant content for advertisers 
while simultaneously responding and tracking expressions of end consumers 
and viewers to generate relevant statistics for the client company, be it on 
customer demography, audience proclivity or attention to bring out real time 
solutions. According to CEO Sachin Garg, “Any display device can work as 
‘magnet,’ if it is built on android. If you have an android device any display can 
be converted into ‘magnet.’ It is hardware agnostic .”(sic) Sundeep Holani, an 
investor in MintM, could not stress enough on the value proposition offered 
by Magnet, “Traditional advertising suffers from a few handicaps – it’s con-
sumed far from the point of purchase, it’s impossible to do in a highly targeted 
way and it’s hard to measure the impact. It(Magnet) beautifully addresses all 
these shortcomings.” (ET Bureau, 2016 ) The renowned clients of MintM have 
seen prominent increase in sales revenue due to enhanced customer engage -
ment and valuable processing of real time customer analytics. MintM relies on 
the fact that the people are more receptive to visual cues and tend to remember 
a brand with video marketing stimuli (Simmonds et al., 2020 ) or comprehend 
a message far better with eye-catching presentations and displays, often lead-
ing to better sales vide visual cues (Pennesi, 2021 ). The video marketing 
stimuli used in MintM were in consonance with products placed in racks. 
MintM happens to be one of the new age firms that seeks to exploit the 
technology whitespace in digital advertising by devising novel software that 
uses algorithm to identify demographics3 [Figure 2], when consumers were 
browsing or dropping by in the store. The data were then securely hosted on 
a cloud-based storage platform, processed, and analyzed to provide a more 
personalized and targeted content according to consumer preferences. This 
was conveniently done through standard 10-inch screen Lenevo tabs fitted 
with the software and placed on aisles of supermarkets and retail stores 
[Figure 3]. The tabs served the dual purpose of showing relevant adverts 
while the device camera recorded and processed real-time data on cloud. 
MintM had a unique value proposition that brands and retailers saw a use 
for. These brands placed the tabs at their product aisle and immensely 
benefitted from the in-depth customer analysis [Figure 4] that MintM was 
able to provide for that last mile targeted advertising. It more-often-than-not

Garg looked after the running of the organization along with product fine- 
tune and innovation aspects, EVP Ashutosh Chouksey led a team for its 
strategic expansion and revenue. The other mainstay of the organization was 
its operations and maintenance team. To offer an exclusive niche value for 
businesses, the organization stressed more on product value creation than 
operations. The lean operations team was a conscious decision that aided in

Implications & IT-related learning objectives
The case is a prime instance of how entrepreneurship, information technology, 
and strategy are blended to form modern businesses. Consequently, the 
challenges and dilemmas that arise in such cases also require a multi-faceted 
synergistic solving. This teaching case has the potential to contribute to 
enhancing IT education from a business perspective. While facing challenges 
and adversity, IT and software enabled business and services has the dynamic 
advantage of fluidity denied to traditional businesses; in case of the former, 
personnel of one team could be deployed into another functional domain as 
per exigencies of business. This is generally not possible in non start-ups with 
strict delimitation of roles. MintM, as a start-up, is armed with additional 
advantages of affording to be much more flexible in terms of organizational 
structure, and business model. This is a significant takeaway while learning 
about software enabled businesses and the challenges they face.
Suggested teaching approach/plan
Learning/teaching objectives
(1)The case provides an avenue to engage students in strategic decision 
making and product/service positioning of information tech-based 
engagements.
(2)The case offers an opportunity to discuss the concepts of business model 
innovation in the back drop of IT and software-based start-ups and 
enable students in designing appropriate business model as per business 
specifics.
(3)The case advocates exploring the choices of persevering with existent 
business plan or pivoting to new plans for new ventures or projects from 
a software and ITES perspective.
Position in course
This case can be used in Entrepreneurship, Innovation and Strategic 
Management, Digital Business Case Study courses/modules at the Graduate/ 
MBA/Post-Graduate level.

Conclusion
MintM revamped their product engineering organization to align with the 
growth strategy. MintM changed into a per tab or per deployment license 
model from the earlier per view revenue model. Post 2018, they only dealt with 
the software and not the tabs/television/other visual conduit. The software is 
deployed and maintained remotely by the same lean operations team. The onus of 
maintenance of medium (i.e. tab/television/display mode) rested on the client i.e. 
the shop owner and/or the retailer brand. Since the operational challenge was 
completely reduced or negated, MintM could expand its business further and 
pursue growth strategy aggressively. Instead of focusing on how many people 
viewed the advert, now the emphasis was on how many licenses the sales team 
sold. Successfully raising 750 USD K in various funding rounds (Crunchbase, 
2020 ), MintM had firmly established itself as a novel start-up offering digital 
signage solutions with geographical presence in Brazil, India, Ireland, Malaysia, 
Singapore, UAE, and USA. MintM was recognized by TIE50 and NASSCOM 
among “50 Most Promising Global Start-ups”. MintM was also able to focus back 
on end products and get new product enhancements and new product lines. 
Subsequently, post 2018, two new products were launched; one on inventory 
creation and maintenance while the other dealt with footfall counting and 
analysis. Both of these have been well received in the market and famed US
