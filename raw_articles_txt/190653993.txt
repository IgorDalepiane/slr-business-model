Abstract A number of service models have been inno-
vated in the software industry so as to meet customers’
special demands and preferences. In order to develop aservice-dominant business model, this project researches
the existing molding simulation software, analyses the
chosen market and industrial users, deﬁnes technicalrequirements, and then categorizes those demands into
different priority levels. Although molding simulation
providers have implemented public cloud services in dif-ferent ways, cloud-based users are not satisﬁed with the
available services provided. Signiﬁcantly, the research
ﬁnding shows that tool builders are not willing to adoptpublic cloud services for molding simulation when there is
a concern about design information protection. To address
this concern and meet the overall demand for simulationrequirements, this project develops a service-dominant
business model which involves how to integrate signiﬁcant
software features and hardware criteria, how to measureservice quality, and what business risks might take place.
The proposed service model is developed according to theadvice offered by people interviewed, and therefore it is
possible to be commercialized.

1.1 Molding simulators
The necessity of employing CAE, Computer-Aided Engi-
neering, software throughout a project workﬂow of the
plastic injection molding process has been understood and
accepted by product developers and tool designers. CAE
software can help users to diagnose and develop complex
injection molding processes [ 4]. If a decision is made by a
trained simulation analyst with the information provided,
the likelihood of making a successful decision is achiev-
able [ 5].
Cloud computing is deﬁned as computing resources
which are accessible without restrictions of places and time
by a normal device which is able to surf the internet [ 6].
This emerging technology allows users to access applica-
tions and computing infrastructure by a means of pay-per-
use or charge-per-use [ 7]. This technique is a suitable so-
lution to analyze critical problems and design optimization
with a number of iterations, and companies, as a result, do
not need to purchase and maintain a high-end computingcluster used for simulation works [ 8].
Except for SIGMASOFT
/C210, Autodesk Moldﬂow and
Moldex3D have both introduced public cloud services tothe market [ 9]. Autodesk A360 is a public cloud which was
built on industrial-standard best practices, and applicants’
ﬁles are kept and backed up in the United States [ 10]. In a
similar way, Moldex3D sells tokens to cloud-based users
so that they can run simulations on the Amazon Web
Services [ 11]. This software package has been providing
enterprise cloud computing service since 2011 [ 12], and its
parallel computing technology allows users to interconnect
a multi-node PC cluster when the license is available.
Despite the advantages mentioned above, Yang and
Hsieh have pointed out that the conﬁdentiality of design
information stored in a public cloud seems debatable [ 13].
Hence, existing running service models need to be
reviewed and modiﬁed so as to meet real demand in
practical.

2.3 Analysis of the existing solutions or products
To enable multiple computations, there are two sales
strategies generally employed. License-oriented sales mean
salespeople do not consider computer hardware in any kind
when they deal with potential buyers for simulation in a
local place. The hardware is chosen based on users’ pref-
erences or recommended by the salesperson. In addition,this hardware system is purchased and installed by the user.
On the other hand, hardware issues are ignored when the
public cloud solution is provided. There are some concernsin terms of this sales strategy.
1. While network security is not a matter to local-based
users, it is a big concern to cloud-based users, denoted
as P1.2. While users can choose a certain hardware system they
prefer, poor integration between software and hard-
ware system might happen which will jeopardizecomputation performance, denoted as P2.
Bundling sales means salespeople promote a package
solution including license and a reliable hardware systemfor local-based users. Moldex3D bundled the Boxcluster in
2008 but the ASUS ESC2000 in 2011 which are examples
of this kind of sales strategy [ 14]. However, there are some
drawbacks.
1. These hardware devices are not developed by Mold-
ex3D. Therefore, it would bring some difﬁculties to the
salespeople when the hardware system malfunctions.
In short, not all sales representatives are willing toemploy this strategy, denoted as P3.
2. While this bundling sales remarkably enhance compu-
tation performance at the customer’s side, salesmargins are comparatively lower, denoted as P4.
3. The maintenance fee of the Boxcluster is too expen-
sive, and it takes a long time to repair the system,denoted as P5.
4. Because computing hardware systems are replaced in
the market at a very quick pace, users, in general, are
unwilling to invest extra equipment for running a
simulation, denoted as P6.

4 Conclusion
While molding simulation providers implement differentservice models, some speciﬁc requirements which arediscussed in this study have not been fully met. In order to
understand customers’ explicit needs, simulation require-
ments are speciﬁcally identiﬁed, categorized, and analyzedin this case study. Based on the research conducted, it
seems public cloud services for molding simulation are not
widely accepted by industrial users in Greater China.Therefore, it is meaningful for this project to propose a
service-dominant business model to solve unsolved prob-
lems and add value to simulation users. This service modelconsiders customers’ needs in data protection particularly
for new design information, integration of signiﬁcant
software functions and hardware criteria, service qualitymeasurement, and business risk aversion. Additionally, this
service model is developed based on the research results
and suggestions made by people interviewed. Conse-quently, it is more likely to create proﬁtable business value
by promoting the proposed service system.
Software service models are signiﬁcantly changed in
recent years so as to meet customers’ special desires and
preferences. As investment cost on a perpetual license is
comparatively higher, the author anticipates that software
developers might terminate this service model to remove
the entry barrier caused by ﬁnancial issues. In order tosatisfy various users, alternative service models are inno-
vated giving rise to ﬂexibility in the way CAE users are
served and charged. These service models are a leasingcontract, a token-based model, an hour-based model, a
component-based model, a computing performance-based
model, or a consulting-based model. The author, as a result,expects that software developers might highly focus on
service model innovation to gain substantial growth.
