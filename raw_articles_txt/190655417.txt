Abstract: Collaborative research project s dedicated to information and 
communication technology and supported by the Framework Program s of the 
European Commission result often in both partnerships among inv olved companies 
and prototypes of common, composite software products. One prob lem of 
commercializing these common produ cts is the transition from a project to a 
commercial setting and the development of a suitable inter-comp any business model. 
In many cases none of the involved companies is able to take ov er the responsibility 
for the common product based on approaches involving joined ven tures, binding 
supplier contracts or buying of licenses from other partners. T hus, flexible business 
models are required that are based on loose partnerships among involved companies, 
but still enable a common presence on the market. By drawing fr om and combining 
aspects from the concepts of value networks and software ecosys tems, this paper 
provides two results: a design procedure and an overview of des ign options for 
development of inter-organizational business models for composi te software products 
based on loose partnerships among involved companies.
1. Introduction
The framework programs (FP) of the European Commission (EC) are one important public initiative with an aim to finance collaborative research. Multi ple exemplary past projects have 
been conducted in the area of information and communication tec hnology (ICT) —the area 
in which the research presented in the paper at hand evolved. B esides research and 
development, ICT FP projects are  increasingly dedicated towards  innovation and 
commercialization of results of collaborative research, typical ly available as prototypes of 
software products consisting of complementary contributions from multiple project partners.Despite the focus on commercial s uccess, individua l partners of ten rate the results of 
collaborative projects, in partic ular the commercialisation and  the direct use of their results 
and related monetary returns, as less successful [1]. Issues to be targeted in this context are 
the shift from a project to a setting with commercial aims, and  the development of inter-
company business models suitable for composite software product s comprising 
complementary components from several independent partners. Spe cific difficulties arise 
from the fact that at the end of the research projects the comm on product might not be mature 
yet. Furthermore, in many cases none of the involved companies is able to take over the 
responsibility for the common product based on approaches invol ving joined ventures, 
subcontracting or buying of licen ses from other partners. Thus,  in these types of projects, a 
mutual commercial future is conceivable only if it is possible to define a common commercial 
setting that, despite being based on loose partnership relation ships among involved software 
companies, still enables a common presence on the market.

Even though existing literature describes different forms of inter-company business
models within the software indus try (e.g., [18; 2; 20; 30]), th ere is a lack of guidelines for 
implementing viable business models that are suitable for the a bove described inter-company 
setting involving both a composite software product and loose p artnerships among involved 
software companies. The paper at  hand aims to provide a contrib ution to filling this gap by 
proposing guidelines for development of inter-company business models suitable for these 
type of settings. By applying th e design science approach [9], two design artefacts (i.e. 
guidelines) are provided: a des ign procedure (DP) suggesting a sequence of design decisions 
that need to be undertaken in the process of business model des ign, and an overview of 
detailed design options (DOs), i.e. design choices for differen t aspects of the respective 
business model.
Deriving from a case study of a FP7 ICT research project, the m ain requirements upon 
collaborative business models are first identified and synthesi zed to a generic research 
problem. Then, based on a broad literature review, and by combi ning components from both 
the concept of value networks (VN) and software ecosystems (SEC O), the two design 
artefacts for inter-company business models for composite software have been developed. 
The two design artefacts have been evaluated t hrough their appl ication in order to develop a 
solution for the chosen FP7 ICT research project.
The content of the paper is structured as follows: section 2 de scribes the applied 
methodology; section 3 describes the results of the four design  science phases; section 4 
provides a summary and discussion of the achieved research resu lts as well as an outlook.
2. Methodology
Driven by the specific case of an FP7 ICT project, the research  problem considered in this 
paper is characterized by a practical nature. Design science re search aims at solving practical 
and theoretical problems by crea ting and evaluating information  technology related artefacts 
(i.e. guidelines) intended to solve identified organizational p roblems [9]. Hence, it is 
considered as a suitable problem-oriented approach [12] for gui ding the research presented 
in this paper. To come to rigorous and relevant research result s, four phases of the design 
science research approach have be en applied as proposed by [9]:
1)Problem Identification and Motivation. In the first phase, the research problem and its 
practical relevance have been specified. Furthermore, the case study of a FP7 ICT 
collaborative research project h as been introduced and the clas s of problems represented 
in it have been identified. Base d on the problem scope, the res earch questions guiding 
this paper have been derived.
2)Define the Objectives for a Solution. In the second phase, the object ives for a solution of 
the problem have been inferred by deducing requirements from th e chosen case and 
aggregating them to generic requi rements which characterise the  identified class of 
similar problems.
3)Design. In the third phase, two design artefacts have been proposed: a design procedure 
(DP) and an overview of design options (DO) for inter-company b usiness models that 
offer composite software products. The design guidelines result ed from a broad literature 
analysis conducted by followi ng the approach proposed by [23]
4)Demonstration and Evaluation. In the fourth phase, the proposed design artefacts have 
been applied to form a solution for the FP7 ICT research projec t. The solution is 
consequently evaluated by the involved research partners.
The results of each of the four  phases are described in detail in the subsequent sections.

3.1 Problem Identification and Motivation
The research problem and motivation will be illustrated on the chosen collaborative ICT FP7 
research project. These types of projects are co-financed by a consortium of public, academic 
and private partners [21]. The i nvolved partners share a common  research interest, contribute 
complementary competences, and work across disciplinary, organi sational and national 
boundaries to create a solution together. The results of these ICT research projects are mainly 
composite software products consisting of complementary contrib utions of independent 
software providers partic ipating in the projects.
The chosen FP7 ICT collaborative research project started in Oc tober 2011 and ended in 
March 2015. The project was inspi red by recent statistics about  new behavioural patterns of

smartphone or tablet while watching TV. One of the most common motives for using a second screen is to search online for additional information re lated to a TV program. Driven

3.3 Design of a Generic Design Procedure and Identification of Design Options
The design artefacts for creating inter-company business models  for a group of independent 
software providers aiming to provide common composite software were developed by 
combining aspects from both VN and SECO. In the first step a br oad literature analysis was 
conducted according to the approach proposed by [23] and related  to design of VN and SECO 
in the software industry. With respect to VN the literature ana lysis revealed that there is a 
small body of literature and only fe w publications related to t he software industry [20]. This 
is probably due to the fact that VN are rather a new concept th at emerged in the late 90s [29]. 
However, based on the existing literature it was possible to ge t first insights about the 
characteristics of value network s [20; 26; 29]. VN are composed  of complementary nodes 
and links [26; 29]. The crucial defining feature of networks is  the complementarity between 
the various nodes and links. A service delivered over a VN requires the contribution of two 
or more network components. Thus , VN can be considered as a set  of relatively autonomous 
units that can be managed independently but operate together in  a framework of common 
principles and service level agreements (SLA) in order to deliv er the value to customers [26]. 
These characteristics of VN match the characteristics of our cas e study and the identified 
generic class of problems. Therefore, the following concepts ca n be drawn from the definition 
and concept of VN for design of the specific inter-company busi ness models considered in 
the paper at hand: specificati on of the contribution of each no de, defining different kinds of 
relationships among the autonomous nodes of the VN, and definin g the framework of 
common cooperation principles based on SLAs. However, in litera ture the VN analysis 
approach is rather used to explore and visualize already existi ng VN. It provides insights 
what needs to be designed, but it does not provide guidelines h ow to design VN. Thus, 
existing literature on VN provides only limited support for dev eloping the solution for the 
class of problems considered in this paper. More detailed design options were identified in 
the literature related to SECO.
According to [19], SECOs are defined “….as a set of businesses functioning as a unit and 
interacting with a shared market  for software and services, tog ether with the relationships 
among them.” A systematic literature analysis was conducted [23 ] also related to the topic of 
SECO design. In total, 88 papers were considered. The analysis of the papers quickly revealed 
that there are no explicit design guidelines for SECOs publishe d yet. The papers either 
defined or described SECOs in general (e.g., [17]), or concentr ated on specific features of 
SECOs (e.g., [4; 6; 12]) or on a certain type of SECO (e.g., [2;  13; 22]). However, as the 
SECO papers focused on different aspects, many of them provided  certain insights and 
suggestions how to build a SECO. These insights complement the findings stemming from 
VN literature.
Overall, given the scattered nature of SECO insights and the va luable complementary 
comprehensions from VN literature, it was necessary to synthesize and combine the various 
findings from literature into generic design artefacts before t hey could be applied for the

DO1 – Common foundation: There are different foundations on the basis of which VN

DO2 – Roles: Defining the roles can help the creation of templates for speci fic business

roles that are also adopted for the DO in this paper are provid ed by the SECO literature.  Most 
of the studied SECO articles in the course of the literature an alysis considered two major 
roles: coordinator and provider. The coordinator is the leading role of a SECO and performs 
the following tasks:
1)Technical coordination - involves providing of a common technological platform [6; 15; 
10, 26]; planning the network ar chitecture and topology; leadin g the development of 
central technology [10; 11; 30] and eventually offering developm ent tools [13; 3; 22].

3.4 Demonstration and Evaluation
The developed DP and DOs were evaluated by applying them for th e design of the inter-
company business model for the considered case. The case specif ic design followed the DP 
presented in Fig. 2. Each step consisted of in-depth discussion  within the consortium until an 
agreement was reached. Firstly, the companies determined what t he common product (DO1) 
of the group was. Secondly, the consortium decided how to distribute the generic roles (DO2) 
among each other. Thirdly, the governance providing the rules f or the cooperation was 
determined (DO3).
According to geographical and customer segmentation of potentia l markets, three distinct 
products that the network would be possible to offer were ident ified: product 1 (P1) is the 
end to end video enrichment solution, consisting of a new data format of enriched video; 
product 2 (P2) is a spec ific end-user applic ation developed wit h the multi-screen toolkit that 
might also include the solution of P1; and product 3 (P3) consi sts of P1 and P2, but as a 
solution bundle includes the dist ribution as a representation b efore the customer. The reasons 
for this differentiation of P3 are differing consistencies in p otential regional markets. All of 
these bundles of software soluti ons are dependent on complement ary contributions of all 
involved partners in their common market.
The partners of the network then proceeded to identify which pa rtners would take over 
specific roles. In particular, it was necessary to determine a w illing and suited partner to take 
over the role of the coordinating partner. From the proposed DO 2 related to the tasks and 
responsibilities of a coordinato r, the consortium selected the following as required: providing 
a “one-face-to-the-customer”; pr oviding marketing and distribut ion for the composite 
products; providing a minimum of technical coordination by keep ing up the dialog and 
notification among partners related to agreed upon APIs and SLA ; coordinating service 
delivery and distribution of pay ments among partners. Two optio ns were considered for the 
choice of a coordinator: to identify a coordinator among the in volved partners, who is willing 
and capable to provide the coordinating activities, or to trans fer all or some coordination 
activities to a new spin-off created by all partners. The invol ved companies rather selected 
several coordinators among themselves. The SME providing the wo rkflow component 
(component 5 in Fig. 1) and having already an established custo mer base in the broadcasting 
industry was considered as capable to take over the central coo rdinating role including 
technical, service and market co ordination for P1. Another SME,  providing the multi-screen 
toolkit took over the market and technical coordinating role fo r P2. A third partner, a 
university providing t he scientific manager, created a spin-off  that provides the market 
coordination for P3 specifically  in Austria. The resulting VN i s illustrated in Fig. 4.

4. Summary and Conclusion
Collaborative research in the software industry, in particular supported by public funding as in case of the FPs of the Europ ean Commission, often results in close partnerships among 
involved companies and composite software products consisting o f complementary 
contributions from involved partners. The commercialisation of such composite software 
products requires a common inter-company business model. Howeve r, in many cases none 
of the companies involved in collaborative research can take over the responsibility and lead for the common product based on approaches involving joined ven tures, binding supplier 
contracts or by way of buying licenses from other partners. Sta rting from the requirements of 
a concrete case of an ICT FP7 collaborative project, the aim of  the paper at hand was the 
extraction of generic guidelines for development of business mo dels for composite software 
products based on loos partnership s of software providers.
By following the design science approach and based on a systematic literature analysis, 
two generic design artefacts we re extracted: a DP and DO for cr eating inter-company 
composite software business model based on loose partnerships o f complementary but 
independent software providers. The DP and DO were applied for finding a practical solution 
for the specific case. The successful application of the design  artefacts to solve a concrete 
real-world problem has proven their practical applicability for  conceptualising an inter-
company business model based on lo ose partnerships. However, if  the defined setting will be 
successful in the future needs to be further evaluated. During the first six months after the 
project in particular the distribution spin-off created by the scientific coordinator presented 
the composite product at a major fair and organized presentatio ns at several broadcasters. It 
can be expected that in case customers can be acquired for the common solutions the loose 
partnerships might evolve into stronger and more binding partne rships. Thus, the only partly 
check of the effectiveness of the design guidelines is one majo r limitation of the provided
