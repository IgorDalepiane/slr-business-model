Abstract
Purpose –The purpose of this paper is to study the adoption and use of open source software (OSS) in the
libraries of Institutions of National Importance (INIs) in India vis-à-vis their economic impact on adopting
libraries. Besides, it aims to study the business model of OSS.
Design/methodology/approach –The paper analyses the adoption and use of OSS in the libraries of
INIs, India, and their economic impact on the adopting libraries. An online survey was administrated to
library professionals of INIs to know the adoption and use of OSS in their libraries and its acceptability.Descriptive statistics and Pearson ’s chi-square are used in this study.
Findings –Theﬁndings of the study reveal that the libraries of INIs adopt and use OSS in integrated library
system (ILS) (18.4 per cent), digital repository (95.2 per cent) and library website (65.5 per cent). Koha, DSpace,
Drupal and Joomla! are the most favoured OSS among the libraries of INIs. The results reveal that favouring of
OSS and having a policy on OSS by the libraries have a positive and signi ﬁcant relationship with their
adoption and use of OSS in the libraries. The results of the study show that university libraries of India couldsave an estimated Rs 417m by adopting open source ILS such as Koha, Evergreen, NewGenLib and ABCD.
Practical implications –The paper will help decision makers to adopt and use open source ILS in their
libraries.
Originality/value –This paper is the ﬁrst of its kind that explores the economic impact of OSS on the
adopting libraries and also explicitly discusses the business model of open source library software.
Keywords India, Academic libraries, Open source software, Economic sustainability,
Business model, Economic implication
Paper type Research paper

2. Literature review
There are a plethora of literature available on the subject of OSS and its adoption in general
and deployment of OSS in library and information centre in particular. A number of studies
have advocated the adoption and use of OSS in enterprises and educational institutions,including libraries.
Raymond (2001) , one of the leading voices and proponent of OSS, claims
that the collaborative methods of developing OSS leads to the development of superior
quality software. OSS communities develop and produce software faster and with fewer
bugs, which in turn are more innovative and cutting edge than the proprietary counterpart.As Raymond, in his seminal essay “The Cathedral and the Bazaar: Musings on Linux and
Open Source by an Accidental Revolutionary ”, has aptly stated:
I think the future of open source software will increasingly belong to people who know how to
play Linus ’s game, people who leave behind the cathedral and embrace the bazaar. This is not to
say that individual vision and brilliance will no longer matter; rather, I think that the cutting edge
of open source software will belong to people who start from individual vision and brilliance, then
amplify it through the e ﬀective construction of voluntary communities of interest ( Raymond,

OSS espouses a philosophy where collaborative development and sharing are inextricably
intertwined. Thousands of top-notched programmers and hackers freely produce highlyqualiﬁed software to the provision of a public good, and they spend much time and efforts
too to improvise the software (
Lakhani and Von Hippel, 2003 ). The emergence of OSS has
raised three economic problems, namely, motivation, coordination and diffusion ( Bonaccorsi
and Rossi, 2003 ). First, the OSS movement took off through the activity of a community that
did not follow pro ﬁt motivations, but it is based on altruism. Second, a hierarchical
coordination emerged sans proprietary rights, i.e. bazaar style of development. Third, OSSdiffused in environments dominated by proprietary standards. They show how recentdevelopments in the theory of diffusion of technologies with network externality may helpto explain these phenomena. OSS phenomenon has metamorphosed into a more mainstreamand commercially viable form, which Fitzgerald (2006) labels as OSS 2.0. He discusses how
the bazaar metaphor, which is up to now has been associated with the OSS development
process, has been actually shifted to become a metaphor better suited to the OSS 2.0 product
delivery and support process. The emergence of OSS accentuates the fundamental alterationof the basic ground rules in the software landscape, signifying the end of the proprietary-driven model that has prevailed for the past 20 years.
In the recent time, OSS is being actively adopted in three regions of the world: Asia, Latin
America and Africa. Asian countries, namely, India, China, Taiwan, South Korea, Malaysia,Singapore and Thailand, are active in the adoption of OSS (
Rajani et al. , 2003 ). Asian
governments are considering “adoption of open source software as a boost for their
economies and a way to increase technological innovation in the region ”(Ribeiro, 2007 ). A
strong governmental support is of paramount importance to increase the adoption of OSS atthe public level. Continuous employees ’training, organisational objectives consensus and
business process reengineering have been found important for the implementation phase
(Rossi et al.,2 0 1 2 ). In India, a good number of projects have been introduced to promote the
adoption of OSS ( Sharma and Adkins, 2006 ).
The adoption and use of OSS in libraries is on a growing spree. With the adoption and
use of various cutting-edge and cost effective OSS in libraries, it provides better services toits users and manipulates large volumes of library data to facilitate instant access toinformation. As the large pool of top-notched programmers are working freely to produce aquality OSS, the features and compatibility between OSS and PS are being reducedgradually. So, libraries are increasingly making use of less costly alternatives to licence-based software (
Payne and Singh, 2010 ;Breeding, 2008 ;Morrissey, 2010 ). Although there
are certain bottlenecks of adopting OSS in libraries, these are social (cultural) disparity,conceptual confusions, digital divide and lack of technological, ﬁnancial and human
development ( Raﬁq and Ameen, 2009 ).

5. Methodology
This paper surveyed the adoption and use of OSS in the libraries of INIs in India and
examined the economic impact of OSS for adopting libraries. The evaluation of the adoption
and use of OSS in the libraries was limited to ILS/library automation. To study the adoption
and use of OSS, an online survey method was used as the population is widely distributed
geographically around all INIs in India. To felicitate the research study, an online survey
questionnaire was designed using LimeSurvey, an OSS survey Web app, and hosted at
www.indianlibrarian.com . The questionnaire has four parts. The ﬁrst part collected
demographic information of the respondents, namely, name, gender, institute name,
designation and experience. The second part gathered information about the type of
institution, budget and staf ﬁng pattern of the library, including the responsibility for
making a decision on the purchase of software in library. The third part of the questionnaire
gathered information about the status of automation of library. The fourth part consist of
questions pertaining to acceptability and receptivity of OSS by the libraries of INIs. The
questionnaire was distributed to 46 libraries of Indian Institute of Management and Indian
Institute of Technology (IITs). A total of 38 responses were received and analysed using
descriptive statistics and Pearson ’s chi-square.

7.1 Economic impact of open source library software
The results of the study show that owing to its performance and robustness, OSS have
been making inroads into the libraries of INIs. It is found that 18.4 per cent of INIs has
adopted open source ILS Koha for computerising the housekeeping operations of their
libraries ( H1). While analysing institution wise, it is revealed that 25 per cent of the
libraries of IIT and 13.6 per cent of libraries of NITs are using open source ILS. In the
case of digital repository software, 95.2 per cent of the libraries of INIs are using OSS
(H2). Majority of INIs libraries have adopted DSpace, though minuscule percentage of
libraries has adopted GSDL and EPrints. The library website is another area whereopen source CMS has been adopted; 34.5 per ce nt of the libraries have adopted Joomla!
and Drupal for library website.

8. Conclusions
This paper studies the adoption and use of OSS in the libraries of INIs in India vis-à-vis their
economic impact on adopting libraries. It focusses on the tangible and intangible bene ﬁts of
OSS. The result shows that the use and adoption of OSS in libraries is an increasing trend,though only 18 per cent of the libraries under the study have adopted open source ILS; and
in case of using open source digital repository and open source CMS, the adoption is found
to be on a meteoric rise. Libraries around the world have adopted OSS for tangible andintangible bene ﬁts. The low TCO, cost saving and avoiding vendor lock-in are the tangible
beneﬁts of OSS adoption, whereas experimentation, sharing of new technology and carefree
use are strong intangible bene ﬁts. The implication of the study shows that university
libraries in India could save an estimated Rs 417m (US$58m) by adopting open source ILSsuch as Koha, Evergreen, NewGenLib and ABCD. Similarly, a huge amount of money can besaved using OSS in other areas of library, e.g. digital repository, library website, electronicresource management and server.
